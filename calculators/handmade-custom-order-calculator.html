Handmade Custom Order Calculator

Price custom work properly: the making time, the design and consultation time buyers often forget, and a rush fee when they need it fast.







—

Formula

Custom order price = (material cost + hourly labor rate × (production hours + design / consultation hours)) × (1 + rush fee ÷ 100). The rush fee is a percentage of the base price, so a rushed order pays more in proportion to the work it displaces.

How it works

Enter material cost, your hourly rate, production hours and the design or consultation hours the custom order needs — messages, sketches, revisions and approvals all count. Add a rush percentage only when the buyer needs faster turnaround than your normal queue. The result separates base price from rush fee so you can explain the quote to the buyer.

Worked example

Material $25.00, labor $30.00 per hour × (3 production + 1 design) hours = $30.00 × 4 = $120.00, base price $25.00 + $120.00 = $145.00. Rush fee 25% of $145.00 = $36.25. Custom order price = $145.00 + $36.25 = $181.25. Interpretation: the $36.25 rush fee is payment for jumping the queue and working under pressure — without it, a rushed order would cost you other work for no extra return.

FAQs

Why charge for design and consultation time?

Custom work starts before making starts: discussing the brief, sketching, sourcing and revisions are skilled work. Leaving them out means the buyer gets that work free and your effective hourly rate falls.

When should I charge a rush fee?

When the buyer needs delivery sooner than your normal turnaround, so you must reorder work, work longer days or delay other orders. State the rush percentage and the delivery date together in the quote.

What if the buyer changes the design halfway through?

Re-run the calculator with the new design hours and material cost, and quote the difference before continuing. Agreeing this rule up front avoids awkward mid-order price changes.

Does this price include platform fees?

No. If the order is sold through a marketplace, gross up the result for that platform's fee, or add the fee separately, so fees do not come out of your labor.

Should every custom order cost more than a standard item?

Usually, yes: one-off design, consultation and the risk of a single buyer not returning all add cost that a repeatable standard item does not carry.
