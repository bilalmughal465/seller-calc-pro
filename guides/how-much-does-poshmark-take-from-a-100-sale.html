How Much Does Poshmark Take From a $100 Sale? A Worked Example for Sellers

Short answer: Poshmark takes $20.00 from a $100 sale — a flat 20% commission — and you keep $80.00 before your item cost. Poshmark's fee has only two tiers: a flat $2.95 on sales under $15, and 20% on sales of $15 or more. There is no listing fee, no monthly fee, and no separate payment-processing charge on top. The table below shows the fee at every common sale size, and the worked example shows the full profit maths on a $100 dress.

Sources: Poshmark's published Fee Policy and fee pages checked September–October 2026 — the $2.95 / 20% structure in effect since 24 October 2024, and the $6.49 buyer-paid shipping label. Poshmark's fees change — check the current Fee Policy in your Poshmark account before you price or list. No sales-tax or income-tax advice is given here.

How the two-tier fee works

Poshmark's US seller fee is one commission with two tiers, restored on 24 October 2024 after a short-lived experiment with a different split (a 5.99% seller fee plus a buyer-side protection fee) that Poshmark dropped within weeks. Under the live structure, sales under $15 pay a flat $2.95 and sales of $15 or more pay 20% of the sale price. Listing is free, so you pay only when something sells. The single fee covers card processing, Poshmark's seller protection (Posh Protect), and a prepaid USPS shipping label for the buyer — which is why there is no separate processing line on your payout. The worked $100 example: a dress, bought for $30

You list a dress for $100.00, a buyer buys it at full price, and you sourced it for $30.00.

Amount
Sale price$100.00
Poshmark fee (20% x $100.00)$20.00
You keep (before item cost)$80.00
Item cost$30.00
Your profit$50.00

Check the maths. The fee is 20% × $100.00 = $20.00. Subtract from the sale price: $100.00 − $20.00 = $80.00. Subtract your $30.00 item cost: $80.00 − $30.00 = $50.00 profit, a 50% margin on the sale price. The buyer also pays $6.49 for shipping on top of the $100.00, but that money goes to the label, not to you and not to the fee. If you had offered a $5.00 shipping discount to close the sale, it would come straight off your payout: $80.00 − $5.00 = $75.00 before your item cost, and $45.00 profit.

Offers change the fee base, not the rate

Poshmark runs on offers, and the commission is charged on the accepted price, not the listing price. List a jacket at $120.00 and accept a $100.00 offer, and the fee is 20% of $100.00 — $20.00 — not 20% of $120.00. The same rule cuts the other way on small sales: accept a $14.00 offer on a $20.00 listing and the flat $2.95 applies instead of 20%, because the accepted price is under $15 — and $2.95 on $14.00 is 21.1%, a worse rate than the 20% tier. Price with the accepted price in mind, not the listing price. The Poshmark Offer Profit Calculator shows what each offer leaves you after the fee.

What else can take a cut

The 20% (or $2.95) is the whole standard fee, but three things can still reduce your payout. A shipping discount you offer comes straight off your payout, as the example above shows. An overweight label upgrade applies when the package is over 5 lb — the buyer's $6.49 label covers up to 5 lb, and the upgrade is deducted from your earnings. Bundle discounts work the same way: a discount you give on a multi-item bundle lowers the accepted price, and the fee is charged on that lower price. None of these is in the $20.00 above, so treat that figure as the fee, not the whole story — the payout is the fee minus anything you chose to give away. Method and limits

Frequently asked questions

How much does Poshmark take from a $100 sale?

Poshmark takes $20.00 from a $100 sale — a flat 20% commission on sales of $15 or more. You keep $80.00 before your item cost. The fee is charged on the accepted sale price only: the buyer pays the $6.49 shipping label separately, and there is no extra payment-processing fee on top of the 20%.

What is Poshmark's seller fee?

Poshmark's US seller fee has two tiers, in effect since 24 October 2024: a flat $2.95 on sales under $15, and a 20% commission on sales of $15 or more. There is no listing fee, no monthly fee, and no separate payment-processing fee — the one commission covers processing, seller protection and the prepaid shipping label.

Does Poshmark charge a payment-processing fee on top of the 20%?

No. The 20% commission already includes payment processing, seller protection and the prepaid USPS shipping label. Nothing else is deducted from your payout except an optional shipping discount you choose to offer, or an overweight label upgrade on packages over 5 lb.

Who pays for shipping on Poshmark?

The buyer pays a flat $6.49 for a USPS Ground Advantage label covering packages up to 5 lb. The shipping charge is not deducted from your $80.00 on a $100 sale. If you offer a shipping discount to win the sale, the discount comes straight off your payout — for example, a $5.00 discount on a $100 sale leaves you $75.00 before your item cost.

What is Poshmark's fee on a $50 sale?

Poshmark takes $10.00 from a $50 sale — 20% of $50.00 — and you keep $40.00 before your item cost. On a $25 sale it takes $5.00 and you keep $20.00; on a $10 sale it takes the flat $2.95 and you keep $7.05.

Is Poshmark or eBay cheaper on a $100 sale?

eBay is cheaper on the fee line. On a $100 sale in most categories eBay takes $14.00 (13.6% plus a $0.40 per-order fee) and you keep $86.00, while Poshmark takes $20.00 and you keep $80.00 — a $6.00 difference. Poshmark's fee includes the shipping label and buyer protection, while on eBay the label and any shipping cost are separate, so compare the full cost, not just the fee, before you choose where to list.
