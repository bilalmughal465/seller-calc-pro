How Much Does eBay Take From a $100 Sale? A Worked Example With a Fee Table

Short answer: eBay takes $14.00 from a $100 sale in most categories — 13.6% of the sale ($13.60) plus the $0.40 per-order fee — and you keep $86.00 before your own costs. That $100 figure assumes no shipping and no sales tax: eBay charges its fee on the total the buyer pays, so shipping, handling and sales tax all raise the fee. With 8% sales tax added, the fee is $15.09 and you keep $84.91 after the tax goes to the state. The table below shows the fee at every common sale size, and the variations section shows exactly what changes the $14.00.

Stand: November 2026. Sources: eBay's published US seller fee pages as reported in October–November 2026 (final value fee 13.6% in most categories, in place since 14 February 2025); seller guides cross-checked in November 2026 (FlowLister, GetOneCart, Taxomate, 3DSellers, PlainSeller). eBay's fee rules change — check the current seller pages before you price or list. No sales-tax or income-tax advice is given here.

Notice the pattern: the per-order fee is a bigger share on small sales. On a $10 sale it is 3% of the price; on a $100 sale it is 0.4%. That is why the total share eBay takes falls as the sale grows — 16.6% of a $10 sale, 14% of a $100 sale, 13.68% of a $500 sale. Above $7,500 the rate drops to 2.35% on the part over $7,500, so the share falls faster still. Run your own numbers in the eBay Final Value Fee Calculator before you list.

The formula, worked on $100

eBay fee = total sale × final value fee rate + per-order fee. For most categories the rate is 13.6% on the first $7,500 of the total sale, then 2.35% on the part above $7,500, rounded to the cent. The "total sale" is more than your price: eBay counts the item price, any handling charge, the shipping the buyer pays, the sales tax, and any other fees the buyer pays.

Worked on $100 with no shipping or tax: 13.6% × $100.00 = $13.60, plus the $0.40 per-order fee = $14.00. You keep $100.00 − $14.00 = $86.00 before your own costs — the item cost, the label, packaging, and payment processing, which are covered in the worked example below.

Worked example: buy for $60, sell for $100

You buy an item for $60.00 and sell it for $100.00, with no shipping charged and no sales tax. eBay takes $14.00, so your payout is $86.00. Subtract the $60.00 you paid for the item and your profit is $26.00 before shipping and packaging costs.

A $50 item with $8 shipping

Shipping changes the fee because it is part of the total sale. A $50.00 item with $8.00 shipping is a $58.00 total sale: 13.6% × $58.00 = $7.888, rounded to $7.89, plus $0.40 = $8.29 taken by eBay, and you receive $49.71. If you had priced the same item at $50.00 with free shipping, the fee would be $7.20 — but you would pay the $8.00 label yourself, so the free-shipping version leaves you worse off unless you raise the price. Compare label prices in the Shipping Cost Comparison Calculator before you choose.

Common mistakes when working out eBay's fee

Pricing from the item price only. The fee is charged on shipping, handling and sales tax too. A $100 item with $10 shipping and tax is a $118.80 fee base, not $100. Forgetting the per-order fee on small sales. On a $10 sale it is $0.30 — 3% of the price. Bundling small items into one order avoids paying it twice. Assuming 13.6% applies everywhere. Clothing pays 15.3%, books and media pay 15.3%, trading cards pay 13.25% — check your category before you price. Ignoring a Below Standard penalty. An extra 6% on the fee turns a $14.00 fee on $100 into $20.00. Paying for a Store too early. The Basic Store saves $0.90 per $100 sale in the categories it covers, but the subscription costs $27.95 a month on the monthly plan — the saving only covers it once your monthly sales are high enough. Treating payment processing as fixed. About 2.7% plus $0.25 is what seller guides report; your own rate may differ slightly, so check your Seller Hub statements. See How to Price for Profit After Fees for how to build all of this into your price.

Method and limits

The standard rate is eBay's published US rule as reported in October–November 2026: final value fee 13.6% of the total sale amount (item price plus shipping plus handling plus sales tax) in most categories, in place since 14 February 2025, up to $7,500, then 2.35% on the part above $7,500; plus a per-order fee of $0.40 on orders over $10.00 and $0.30 on orders of $10.00 or less. Category rates differ — clothing, books and media pay 15.3%, trading cards pay 13.25% — and the full list is in the eBay Fees by Category guide. Basic Store sellers pay 12.7% in many categories from the Basic level; Top Rated Plus gives 10% off the final value fee on qualifying listings; a Below Standard rating adds 6% to the fee; an international buyer adds 1.65% unless you use eBay International Shipping. Payment processing of about 2.7% plus $0.25 is an approximate figure as reported by seller guides, not a fixed published rate for every seller. The fee table excludes shipping and sales tax, so on a taxed or shipped sale every fee line is higher, as the variations section shows. eBay can change its fees at any time — always check eBay's current seller pages before you price, promote or list.

Frequently asked questions

How much does eBay take from a $100 sale?

In most categories, eBay takes $14.00 from a $100 sale with no shipping or sales tax: 13.6% of $100.00 is $13.60, plus the $0.40 per-order fee. You keep $86.00 before your own costs. If the buyer pays shipping or sales tax, the fee is charged on that total too, so the fee is higher.

Does eBay charge fees on shipping?

Yes. eBay's final value fee of 13.6% in most categories is charged on the total the buyer pays — item price plus shipping plus handling plus sales tax — not just the item price. On a $50 item with $8.00 shipping, the fee is 13.6% of $58.00, which is $7.89, plus $0.40, so eBay takes $8.29. Charging more for shipping also raises the fee on it.

Does eBay charge fees on sales tax?

Yes. The sales tax eBay collects is part of the total sale, so the final value fee is charged on it. On a $100 item with 8% sales tax, the fee base is $108.00, the fee is 13.6% of $108.00, which is $14.69, plus $0.40, so $15.09 in total. The tax itself goes to the state, so after the tax is passed on you keep $84.91.

How much does eBay take from a $10 sale?

eBay takes $1.66 from a $10 sale in most categories: 13.6% of $10.00 is $1.36, plus the $0.30 per-order fee that applies to orders of $10.00 or less. You keep $8.34. The per-order fee is a bigger share on small sales — on a $10 sale it is 3% of the price, against 0.4% on a $100 sale.

Do eBay Store sellers pay less?

From the Basic Store level, many categories pay 12.7% instead of 13.6%. On a $100 sale that is $12.70 plus $0.40, so $13.10 instead of $14.00 — a saving of $0.90 per sale. The Basic Store costs $27.95 a month on the monthly plan ($21.95 on the yearly plan), so the saving only covers the subscription once your monthly sales in those categories are high enough.

How much does eBay take if I am a Top Rated seller?

Top Rated sellers get 10% off the final value fee on listings that qualify for Top Rated Plus. On a $100 sale, 13.6% becomes 12.24%, so eBay takes $12.24 plus the $0.40 per-order fee, which is $12.64 instead of $14.00. The discount does not cover the $0.40 per-order fee. Qualifying needs same-day or 1-business-day handling and free returns for at least 30 days.

Does eBay take a cut if my item does not sell?

No. There is no final value fee if nothing sells. Most sellers get 250 free listings a month, so many pay nothing at all to list. After that, each listing costs $0.35 and is not refunded if the item does not sell. Optional upgrades, such as a reserve price on an auction, are also charged whether or not the item sells.
