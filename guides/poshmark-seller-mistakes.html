Poshmark Seller Mistakes: 7 Costly Mistakes New Sellers Make

Short answer: The most expensive mistake is pricing without the fee: list a dress at $24 wanting to keep $24 and you keep $19.20, because Poshmark takes 20% on sales of $15 or more. The next three cost just as much in quiet ways — the flat $2.95 fee on cheap items, Offer to Likers discounts that come with a shipping discount you fund, and label upgrades on parcels over 5 lb. The last three cost you sales instead of dollars: vague listings that open return cases, slow shipping, and listing once and disappearing. Every mistake below is priced in dollars where it can be, and every one has a fix.

Mistake 1: Pricing to keep, not pricing to list

This is the mistake that hits first, because it hits on the very first sale. A new seller decides a dress is worth $24 to them, lists it at $24, and expects $24. Poshmark takes 20% of $24.00, which is $4.80, so the payout is $19.20 — $4.80 short of what they planned. The fix is to price backwards from what you want to keep. To keep $24.00 on a sale of $15 or more, divide by 0.80: $24.00 ÷ 0.80 = $30.00. List at $30.00, Poshmark takes 20% x $30.00 = $6.00, and you keep $24.00. The same maths works at any price: to keep $40.00, list at $50.00, because 20% x $50.00 = $10.00. Run the number before you list, not after the payout lands — see the Poshmark Fee Calculator for the exact keep on any price.

Mistake 2: Ignoring the under-$15 flat fee

Under $15, Poshmark does not take 20% — it takes a flat $2.95, and on a cheap item that flat fee is a bigger bite than 20% would be. A $12 top keeps $12.00 − $2.95 = $9.05, so the fee is $2.95 ÷ $12.00 = 24.6% of the price. A $10 item keeps $7.05, a 29.5% fee. The fix is to bundle cheap items instead of selling them one by one. Three $12 tops sold separately keep 3 x $9.05 = $27.15, with $8.85 paid in fees. The same three tops bundled at $36.00 pay one 20% fee of $7.20 and keep $28.80 — $1.65 more, and the buyer pays one $6.49 label instead of three. Check the bundle maths in the Bundle Profit Calculator before you list a pile of low-priced items.

Mistake 3: Offers that eat the profit

Poshmark is a negotiation marketplace, and buyers expect offers — but every offer has three moving parts, and new sellers count only one. An Offer to Likers must be at least 10% below the list price, it lasts 24 hours, and it includes a shipping discount that comes out of your earnings. Take a $40 jacket. A 10% offer brings it to $36.00. Poshmark takes 20% x $36.00 = $7.20. Offer $4.99 shipping and you cover $1.50 of the $6.49 label. You keep $36.00 − $7.20 − $1.50 = $27.30, against $32.00 if it had sold at full price — the offer costs $4.70. Offer free shipping instead and you cover the whole $6.49 label, keeping $36.00 − $7.20 − $6.49 = $22.31. The fix is to price the offer before you send it: list 10–15% above your walk-away price so the offer lands where you want, and check the keep in the Poshmark Offer Profit Calculator first. If a buyer counters, the shipping discount drops off — but the price cut stays.

Mistake 4: Overweight parcels over 5 lb

The buyer-paid $6.49 label covers parcels up to 5 lb. Over that, you pay to upgrade the label, and the upgrade comes out of your earnings: $5.00 for 5.01–10 lb and $10.00 for 10.01–15 lb. A $25 coat that weighs 7 lb costs a $5.00 fee plus a $5.00 upgrade, so you keep $25.00 − $5.00 − $5.00 = $15.00 — the upgrade takes a fifth of the sale. Heavy items are where this mistake bites hardest: boots, coats, bundles and home goods. The fix is to weigh before you list, not after it sells. If the parcel is over 5 lb, either price the upgrade in or skip the item — a $15 sale on a 7 lb parcel keeps $15.00 − $3.00 − $5.00 = $7.00, which is rarely worth the trip to the post office. See the Poshmark shipping checklist for the full weight rules.

Mistake 5: Vague photos and descriptions

Buyers cannot touch the item, so the listing does all the work — and a vague listing is also your only defence if a case is opened. Poshmark returns are only accepted when the item is significantly not as described, so an undisclosed flaw is the most common reason a case is opened, and a lost case means a refund and a lost sale. The fix is specific, not long: bright natural light, a clean background, front, back and close-ups of labels and any flaw, and a description with brand, size, colour, material, exact measurements and the item's condition in plain words (NWT, NWOT, or the flaw itself). A flaw photographed and disclosed rarely causes a case; the same flaw discovered by the buyer almost always does. This is the one mistake on this list where the fix costs nothing but a few minutes.

Mistake 6: Slow shipping

Poshmark expects sellers to ship within two days of a sale, and buyers notice when a label sits unscanned. Late shipping can lead to cancellations, and it hurts your seller rating, which is the trust signal the next buyer reads before they buy. The fix is to prepare before you list: keep poly mailers, boxes, tissue and a scale by the door, print the label the morning after the sale, and drop the parcel the same day. A sale that ships in one day and a sale that ships in four days pay the same fee — but only the first one builds the rating that brings the next sale. If you know you cannot ship for a few days, say so in the listing or wait to list until you can.

Mistake 7: Listing and disappearing

Poshmark is a social marketplace, not a shelf. Listings that are never shared sink in search, and a closet with no activity looks abandoned to buyers. The fix is a short daily routine, not hours of work: share your own listings to your followers and to Posh Parties, answer buyer questions the same day, and send an Offer to Likers when an item collects likes. Sharing refreshes a listing's place in search results and feeds, and a prompt reply is often what turns a liker into a buyer. The sellers who treat the closet like a shop that opens every day are the ones whose items keep moving; the ones who list once and wait are the ones who wonder why nothing sells.

The worked example: a $40 jacket, three ways

One jacket, listed at $40.00, shows how mistakes 1 and 3 stack. Sold at full price, Poshmark takes 20% x $40.00 = $8.00 and you keep $32.00. Sent as a 10% Offer to Likers at $36.00 with $4.99 shipping, the fee is 20% x $36.00 = $7.20, the shipping discount costs you $1.50, and you keep $36.00 − $7.20 − $1.50 = $27.30. The same offer with free shipping costs the full $6.49 label, and you keep $36.00 − $7.20 − $6.49 = $22.31. The gap between the best and worst outcome on the same jacket is $32.00 − $22.31 = $9.69 — nearly a quarter of the list price, decided entirely by the offer and the shipping choice. Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest mistake new Poshmark sellers make?

Pricing without the fee. New sellers list at the price they want to keep, then Poshmark takes 20% on sales of $15 or more — or a flat $2.95 under $15 — and the payout lands short. List a dress at $24 wanting to keep $24 and you keep $19.20. To keep $24 you must list at $30, because 20% of $30 is $6.00. Price for the fee first, then list.

How much does Poshmark take from a $20 sale?

Poshmark takes 20% of a $20 sale, which is $4.00, so you keep $16.00. The buyer pays the $6.49 flat shipping label separately, so shipping does not come out of your $16.00 unless you offered a shipping discount or the parcel weighs over 5 lb.

Do shipping discounts come out of the seller's earnings on Poshmark?

Yes. Any shipping discount you offer comes straight out of your earnings. Offering $4.99 shipping instead of the standard $6.49 costs you $1.50, and offering free shipping costs you the full $6.49. On a $40 jacket sold at a 10% offer of $36 with $4.99 shipping, you keep $27.30 instead of $32.00 — the offer and the shipping discount together cost $4.70.

What happens if my Poshmark package weighs over 5 lb?

You pay to upgrade the label, and the upgrade comes out of your earnings: $5.00 for a parcel of 5.01 to 10 lb, and $10.00 for 10.01 to 15 lb. A $25 coat that weighs 7 lb costs a $5.00 fee plus a $5.00 label upgrade, so you keep $15.00. Weigh heavy items before you list, or the upgrade eats the profit.

Can a buyer return an item on Poshmark?

Only if the item is significantly not as described. Buyer's remorse and fit problems are generally not valid reasons for a return when measurements were provided. That is why vague descriptions and hidden flaws are so costly: an undisclosed flaw is the most common reason a case is opened, and a lost case means a refund and a lost sale.

How fast do I need to ship on Poshmark?

Poshmark expects sellers to ship within two days of a sale. Slow shipping can lead to cancellations and hurts your seller rating, so keep mailers and boxes ready before you list, not after something sells.
