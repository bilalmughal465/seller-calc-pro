What Is the Cheapest Way to Ship a Small Package as a Seller?

Short answer: for most small packages under 1 lb, USPS Ground Advantage bought online at commercial rates is the cheapest tracked option — an 8 oz package to Zone 5 costs $4.91 online against $7.85 at the post office counter. The service is the same; only where you buy the label changes the price. Two exceptions: books and media go cheaper by USPS Media Mail, and trading cards or stamps go cheaper by eBay Standard Envelope, often under $1. The table below compares every option by weight, and the worked example shows what the label choice does to a $30 sale.

The retail trap: the same label costs $2.94 more at the counter

The single biggest shipping saving most new sellers miss is not a service — it is where the label is bought. The post office counter charges retail rates; eBay labels, Pirate Ship and USPS.com charge commercial rates for the same label on the same package. The gap is largest on light packages.

PackageRetail (counter)Commercial (online)You save
8 oz Ground Advantage, Zone 5$7.85$4.91$2.94 (37.5%)
2 lb Priority Mail, Zone 5$14.85$11.88$2.97 (20.0%)

Ship 100 eight-ounce packages a month and the counter costs you $294.00 extra — $2.94 × 100 — for nothing at all. Pirate Ship charges no monthly fee and no per-label fee; it is paid by the carriers, and it passes through USPS Commercial rates and negotiated UPS rates. eBay labels are just as cheap for straightforward shipments and are one click from the sold listing, but Pirate Ship wins on two things: it shows Priority Mail Cubic pricing automatically when that is cheaper, and its UPS rates are often $1-3 lower. Note the commercial rate for the same weight varies slightly by platform and exact zone — the 8 oz Zone 5-6 commercial rate runs about $4.30 to $4.91 — so treat the table as a guide and check the quote for your buyer's zone.

The worked example: a $30 t-shirt, 8 oz, Zone 5

You sell a t-shirt for $30.00 on eBay. It weighs 8 oz packed in a poly mailer, the buyer is in Zone 5, and the buyer pays the shipping you charge.

Amount
Sale price$30.00
Shipping charged to buyer$4.91
Total the buyer pays$34.91
eBay fee (13.6% × $34.91 + $0.40)$5.15
You receive$29.76
Ground Advantage label (commercial)$4.91
Poly mailer$0.12
You keep before item cost$24.73

Check the maths. The eBay fee is 13.6% × $34.91 = $4.75, plus the $0.40 per-order fee = $5.15 — eBay charges its percentage on the shipping the buyer pays too, not just the item price. You receive $34.91 − $5.15 = $29.76. The label costs $4.91 and the poly mailer about $0.12, so you keep $29.76 − $4.91 − $0.12 = $24.73 before your item cost. Now buy the same label at the counter for $7.85: you keep $29.76 − $7.85 − $0.12 = $21.79 — exactly $2.94 less, the whole retail-vs-commercial gap, straight out of your profit. Three special cases that beat Ground Advantage

Books and media — Media Mail. USPS Media Mail is the cheapest option if the item qualifies: books, CDs, DVDs, sheet music, comics and magazines. It does not apply to electronics, clothing or non-media bundles. Commercial single-piece Media Mail starts at $4.47 for 1 lb and $5.22 for 2 lb (rates from 5 July 2026). A 2 lb book costs $5.22 by Media Mail against $6.60 by Ground Advantage to Zones 5-6 — a $1.38 saving. eBay labels do not offer Media Mail, so buy the label through Pirate Ship or Stamps.com.

Trading cards and stamps — eBay Standard Envelope. The tracked envelope service for low-value flat items often costs under $1, against $3.30-plus for a Ground Advantage label on the same card. It only works for flat items that fit a standard envelope and meet eBay's value and size limits, so it is a cards-and-stamps option, not a general small-package service.

Small, heavy, dense items — Priority Mail Cubic. Cubic pricing charges by the box's size, not its weight, for packages under 20 lb and 0.50 cubic feet. A heavy cast iron pan in a 12" × 10" × 4" box is 480 cubic inches ÷ 1,728 = 0.28 cubic feet, tier 0.3: about $13.70 to Zone 7 against about $22.00 by regular Priority Mail for its 8 lb weight — an $8.30 saving. You cannot select cubic pricing at the post office; it is only available through commercial platforms, and Pirate Ship shows it automatically when it is cheaper. Use the smallest box possible — cubic pricing rewards compact packing. Check cubic eligibility in the Dimensional Weight Calculator.

Two things that quietly raise the price: dimensional weight and packaging. Carriers charge by size when a box is large and light — a 4 lb item in an 18" × 14" × 12" box is billed at 22 lb by UPS and FedEx ((18 × 14 × 12) ÷ 139 = 21.8 lb) and 19 lb by USPS ((18 × 14 × 12) ÷ 166 = 18.2 lb, over 1 cubic foot). Ground Advantage is not subject to dimensional weight for packages under 1 cubic foot, and poly mailers have no dimensional weight at all — one more reason to use them for anything soft. And a Flat Rate box only helps when the item is heavy for its size: a 6 oz item in a Flat Rate Envelope costs $7.75 when Ground Advantage would cost $3.70. Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to ship a small package as a seller?

For most small packages under 1 lb, USPS Ground Advantage bought online at commercial rates is the cheapest tracked option — an 8 oz package to Zone 5 costs $4.91 online against $7.85 at the post office counter. Buy the label through eBay labels or Pirate Ship, never at the counter, and ship soft items in a poly mailer. Two exceptions: books and media go cheaper by USPS Media Mail ($4.47 for 1 lb), and trading cards or stamps go cheaper by eBay Standard Envelope, often under $1.

How much does it cost to ship an 8 oz package?

An 8 oz package to Zone 5 costs $4.91 with a USPS Ground Advantage label bought online at commercial rates, and $7.85 for the same label bought at the post office counter — a $2.94 difference on one package. Across Zones 5-6 the commercial rate runs about $4.30 to $4.91, and across all zones about $3.90 to $4.60. The exact price depends on the destination zone and which label platform you use, so check the zone before you list.

Is Pirate Ship really free?

Yes — Pirate Ship charges no monthly fee and no per-label fee. It is paid by the carriers, not by you, and it passes through USPS Commercial rates and negotiated UPS rates. It also shows Priority Mail Cubic pricing automatically when that is cheaper, which eBay labels does not always do. The postage itself is never free: you still pay the carrier for every label.

What is the cheapest way to ship a book?

USPS Media Mail is the cheapest way to ship a book, if the item qualifies — books, CDs, DVDs, sheet music, comics and magazines, but not electronics, clothing or non-media bundles. Commercial single-piece Media Mail starts at $4.47 for 1 lb and $5.22 for 2 lb (rates from 5 July 2026). A 2 lb book costs $5.22 by Media Mail against $6.60 by Ground Advantage to Zones 5-6, a $1.38 saving. eBay labels do not offer Media Mail, so buy the label through Pirate Ship or Stamps.com.

Can I ship a trading card for under $1?

Yes, with eBay Standard Envelope — the tracked envelope service for low-value flat items such as trading cards and stamps. It often costs under $1, far below the $3.30-plus a Ground Advantage label would cost for the same card. It only works for flat items that fit a standard envelope and meet eBay's value and size limits, so it is a cards-and-stamps option, not a general small-package service.

Is USPS Ground Advantage cheaper than UPS for small packages?

Yes, for small packages. Ground Advantage at commercial rates beats UPS Ground and FedEx Ground up to about 5 lb — a 1 lb package to Zones 5-6 costs $5.40 by Ground Advantage against about $8.30 by Priority Mail, and UPS and FedEx Ground only become competitive around 3-5 lb. Above about 10 lb the picture flips: a 10 lb package costs about $13.00-$13.50 by UPS or FedEx Ground against about $21.00 by USPS Priority Mail to Zones 5-6, so compare all three carriers on anything heavy.
