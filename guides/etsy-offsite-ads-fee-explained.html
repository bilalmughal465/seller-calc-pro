Etsy Offsite Ads Fee Explained: When the 12% (or 15%) Actually Applies

Short answer: Etsy charges an Offsite Ads fee only when a buyer clicks an Etsy-purchased ad for your listing and then buys from your shop within 30 days. The rate is 15% if your shop made under $10,000 in the past 365 days, and 12% — mandatory, with no opt-out — once your shop has made $10,000 or more in a 365-day period. The fee is charged on the item price plus the shipping the buyer pays, on top of every other Etsy fee, and it is capped at $100 per order. On the $40 sale worked out below, the 15% fee is $6.75, and it cuts what you keep from $22.27 down to $15.52.

Stand: November 2026. Sources: Etsy's Offsite Ads policy as reported by current seller guides. Fee rules change — check Etsy's current Offsite Ads page and your Shop Manager settings before you price. No sales-tax or income-tax advice is given here.

Worked example: one $40 sale, with and without the fee

A US sale: a $40 item, $5 shipping charged to the buyer, a $5 shipping label you buy, a $12 item cost and $1 packaging. Etsy fees used here are the standard US stack from the Etsy Fees Explained guide: a $0.20 listing fee, a 6.5% transaction fee and a 3% + $0.25 payment-processing fee, all on the $45 order total, plus the Offsite Ads fee where it applies.

VersionAll Etsy feesYou keep
No offsite-ad sale$0.20 + $2.93 + $1.60 = $4.73$22.27
Offsite-ad sale, under $10,000 shop (15%)$4.73 + $6.75 = $11.48$15.52
Offsite-ad sale, $10,000+ shop (12%)$4.73 + $5.40 = $10.13$16.87

Check the maths on the middle row. The transaction fee is 6.5% x $45 = $2.93 (rounded), the processing fee is 3% x $45 + $0.25 = $1.60, and the Offsite Ads fee is 15% x $45 = $6.75. Total fees are $0.20 + $2.93 + $1.60 + $6.75 = $11.48. Then $45.00 - $11.48 - $12.00 item - $5.00 label - $1.00 packaging = $15.52. The 12% row uses 12% x $45 = $5.40 instead, giving $16.87. The fee is the single biggest line on an attributed sale — bigger than the transaction and processing fees combined — which is why the five mistakes below cost real money.

5 mistakes that turn the fee into a loss

1. Pricing as if the fee does not exist. A price that leaves a healthy margin after the standard fees can still lose money on an attributed sale, because the Offsite Ads fee alone is $6.75 on a $45 order. Price with the attributed-sale case in mind, not just the ordinary case. The pricing guide shows how to build that margin in.

2. Charging the fee on the item price only. The fee base is the item price plus the shipping the buyer pays. On this sale, 15% of $40 would be $6.00; the real fee is 15% of $45, which is $6.75. The $0.75 gap is small here and grows with every dollar of shipping you charge.

3. Assuming you can always opt out. Under $10,000, you can opt out in Shop Manager — and for thin-margin shops, opting out is often the right call. But once your shop crosses $10,000 in a 365-day period, participation is mandatory for the lifetime of the shop, and the 12% rate applies to attributed sales from then on.

4. Thinking only the clicked listing counts. The 30-day window covers purchases from your shop, not just the listing in the ad. A buyer who clicks an ad, browses, and buys a different item from your shop a week later still triggers the fee on that order.

5. Expecting the fee to drop back or the opt-out to return if sales fall. Once mandatory, the shop stays mandatory at 12%, even if later sales fall back below $10,000. Plan prices for the 12% case as a permanent cost, not a temporary one — and remember the $100 cap only helps on very large orders: 15% passes $100 only once an order passes about $667.

Method and limits

Figures use the published US rules as reported in November 2026: a 15% Offsite Ads fee under $10,000 in the past 365 days, 12% and mandatory at $10,000 or more, charged on item price plus buyer-paid shipping, capped at $100 per order, triggered only by a purchase within 30 days of an ad click. The standard fee stack in the example is a $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee and 3% + $0.25 payment processing, matching SellerCalc's existing Etsy guides. Examples exclude sales tax, ads you buy yourself, and any regulatory or currency-conversion charges, and treat the shipping label you buy as equal to the shipping you charge. Thresholds and rates change — always check Etsy's current Offsite Ads page before you price.

Frequently asked questions

When does the Etsy Offsite Ads fee drop from 15% to 12%?

When your shop has made $10,000 or more on Etsy in the past 365 days. Under $10,000, the fee is 15% and you can opt out. At $10,000 or more, the fee is 12% and participation is mandatory for the lifetime of the shop.

Can I opt out of Etsy Offsite Ads?

Only while your shop has made less than $10,000 in the past 365 days. Once your shop crosses $10,000 in a 365-day period, Offsite Ads become mandatory and you cannot opt out, even if your sales later fall back below $10,000.

Is the Etsy Offsite Ads fee charged on the item price only, or on shipping too?

On the item price plus the shipping the buyer pays. On a $40 item with $5 shipping charged, the 15% fee is charged on $45, which is $6.75, not on $40.

What if the buyer purchases more than 30 days after clicking the ad?

Then no Offsite Ads fee is charged on that order. The fee only applies when the buyer purchases from your shop within 30 days of clicking an offsite ad. After the 30-day window closes, the order is not attributed to the ad.

Is there a maximum Etsy Offsite Ads fee per order?

Yes. The Offsite Ads fee is capped at $100 per order. On a very large order, 15% of the total would pass $100, but Etsy charges at most $100 for that order's Offsite Ads fee.

Do I pay the Offsite Ads fee if nobody buys?

No. Etsy charges the Offsite Ads fee only when a buyer clicks an offsite ad and then purchases from your shop within 30 days. Clicks with no purchase cost you nothing under this program.
