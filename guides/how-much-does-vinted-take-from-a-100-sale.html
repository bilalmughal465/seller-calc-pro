How Much Does Vinted Take From a $100 Sale? $0 Seller Fee, the Buyer Fee, and the October 2026 Rename

Short answer: Vinted takes $0.00 from a $100 sale — you keep the full $100.00. Vinted charges sellers no listing fee, no commission, and no payment-processing fee. The buyer pays the platform instead, through a mandatory Vinted fee at checkout — usually 5% + $0.70, which is $5.70 on a $100 item — plus shipping. None of that comes out of your $100.00. The formula

Seller fee = $0.00 — no listing fee, no commission, no payment-processing fee

What you keep = the item price, in full

Buyer Vinted fee (usual) = 5% of the item price + $0.70 — paid by the buyer, not you

Vinted is the only major US resale marketplace in this guide series with a 0% seller take rate. Its help page says it plainly: "There are no fees for uploading your items or selling them on Vinted. This means once your sale is successfully closed, you'll receive the full selling price straight to your Vinted Wallet." The platform is paid from the other side of the transaction — the buyer pays a mandatory Vinted fee at checkout, plus shipping. That split is the whole story of this guide: the seller's maths is trivially simple, and the buyer's fee is what decides whether the sale actually happens.

Worked example 1: a $100 item, buyer pays shipping

A $100 jacket, standard sale, buyer pays shipping, no optional promotion.

Seller fee. $100.00 × 0% = $0.00. No listing fee, no commission, no processing fee.

What you keep. $100.00 — the full item price, a 0.0% take rate.

What the buyer pays. $100.00 item + $5.70 usual Vinted fee (5% × $100.00 = $5.00, + $0.70) = $105.70 before shipping. Shipping is added on top, paid by the buyer. Vinted generates a prepaid shipping label for you, so your only cost is packaging.

Worked example 2: the profit after the item cost

You buy a jacket at a thrift store for $40 and sell it for $100 on Vinted, buyer pays shipping.

You keep. $100.00 — no fees deducted.

Profit. $100.00 − $40.00 item cost = $60.00 profit.

Compare that with the same flip on Poshmark, where the 20% fee takes $20.00 and the same $40 item leaves $40.00 profit. The $20.00 gap is the whole reason resellers cross-list on Vinted. If you choose to subsidise shipping yourself — say $7.00 — the profit is $100.00 − $40.00 − $7.00 = $53.00. Vinted does not require you to subsidise shipping, so treat that $7.00 as a choice, not a fee. Worked example 3: the same sale with a paid Item Bump

The same $100 item, but you pay to promote it with an Item Bump for extra visibility.

Item Bump cost. Typically $0.75 to $3.00 depending on the item and the market — take $2.00 as the example.

You keep. $100.00 − $2.00 = $98.00.

Item Bump is optional and is the only seller-facing cost Vinted has: it pushes one listing higher in search and category feeds for 3 or 7 days. It is never required to list or sell, so a $100 sale with no bump still pays $100.00. Only buy a bump when the extra visibility is worth more than the $2.00 — a slow-moving item at $100 is a better bump candidate than a fast seller at $15.

Worked example 4: a two-item bundle totalling $100

A buyer purchases two items from your wardrobe in one order — $60.00 and $40.00, totalling $100.00.

Seller fee. Still $0.00. You keep $100.00.

Buyer Vinted fee (usual). Vinted says the fee varies by whether the order is a single item or a bundle, so the bundle fee is not guaranteed to equal the single-item formula. On the usual formula it would be 5% × $100.00 + $0.70 = $5.70, and the buyer pays $105.70 before shipping — but the exact bundle fee is set at checkout, so tell a bundle buyer to check the checkout total rather than quoting $5.70 as fixed.

The October 2026 rename: Buyer Protection fee became the Vinted fee

Until October 2026 the buyer-side charge was called the Buyer Protection fee. Under Vinted's updated terms from 8 October 2026 it is now called the Vinted fee. Nothing about the maths changed — it is the same mandatory buyer charge, usually 5% + $0.70 — only the name changed. If you read an older guide that mentions Buyer Protection, it is talking about the same fee. Two things about the fee are worth knowing:

"Usually" is doing real work. Vinted's own wording is that the fee is usually 5% + $0.70. The exact amount varies by the item's characteristics, the order value, and whether the order is a single item or a bundle — and it is set at checkout. Treat 5% + $0.70 as the usual case, never as a guaranteed formula.

The fixed $0.70 weighs heaviest on cheap items. Because $0.70 is fixed, the usual fee is about 12% of a $10 item but only about 5.7% of a $100 item. A buyer sees $1.20 on a $10 purchase and $5.70 on a $100 purchase — the fee feels much bigger on the cheap item, which is one reason low-priced listings can stall at checkout even though the seller keeps the full price.

Five mistakes that eat the Vinted margin

Pricing as if the buyer fee does not exist. You keep $100.00, but the buyer pays $105.70 before shipping. A buyer who expected $100 at checkout can abandon the sale — price with the checkout total in mind, not just your payout. See the seller fees comparison calculator for the full picture.

Treating Vinted like a business channel. Vinted prohibits selling as a business, bulk resale, dropshipping, and made-to-order items. A business account or bulk-resale pattern risks the account — use eBay or Etsy for business selling instead.

Paying for bumps on items that sell themselves. A $2.00 Item Bump on a fast-selling $100 item is $2.00 of pure cost. Save bumps for listings that have sat unsold, not for items that move in a day.

Forgetting the payout is not instant. The sale price lands in your Vinted Wallet when the sale closes, but the buyer has about 2 days after delivery to confirm receipt, and the funds become available only after that window. Do not count on the money the day the item ships.

Pricing without the item cost. $100.00 kept is not $100.00 profit — subtract what you paid for the item first, as in worked example 2. Use the Break-Even Calculator to check the real margin before you list.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Vinted take from a $100 sale?

Vinted takes $0.00 from a $100 sale — you keep the full $100.00. Vinted charges sellers no listing fee, no commission, and no payment-processing fee. The buyer pays a separate Vinted fee at checkout, usually 5% + $0.70, which is $5.70 on a $100 item, plus shipping — none of it comes out of your $100.00.

Does Vinted charge sellers any fees?

No. Vinted charges sellers no listing fee, no commission, and no payment-processing fee. Vinted's own help page says: "There are no fees for uploading your items or selling them on Vinted" and that once a sale closes you receive the full selling price straight to your Vinted Wallet. The only seller costs are optional promotion tools such as Item Bump.

What is the Vinted fee that buyers pay?

The Vinted fee is the mandatory fee buyers pay at checkout. Vinted's price list says it is usually 5% of the item price plus $0.70, but the exact amount varies by item, order value, and whether the order is a single item or a bundle. On a $100 item the usual fee is $5.70, so the buyer pays $105.70 before shipping. It was called the Buyer Protection fee until Vinted renamed it in October 2026.

Do sellers pay for shipping on Vinted?

No. The buyer pays shipping on top of the item price and the Vinted fee. Vinted generates a prepaid shipping label for the seller, and the buyer covers the shipping cost at checkout. The seller's only shipping cost is packaging the item — unless the seller chooses to subsidise shipping, which Vinted does not require.

When do Vinted sellers get paid?

When a sale closes, the full selling price goes to your Vinted Wallet. The balance shows as pending while the buyer has about 2 days after delivery to confirm receipt and check the item; if no claim is opened, the funds become available, typically about 2 days after delivery confirmation, and can then be withdrawn to a bank account.

Can I sell as a business on Vinted?

No. Vinted is for individuals selling second-hand items. Its rules prohibit selling as a business or on behalf of one, reselling items bought in bulk, dropshipping, and selling custom made-to-order items. Sellers who run a business should use a marketplace built for businesses, such as eBay or Etsy, instead.
