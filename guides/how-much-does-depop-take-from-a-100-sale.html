How Much Does Depop Take From a $100 Sale? A Worked Example for Sellers

Short answer: Depop takes $3.75 from a $100 sale for a US seller — 3.3% of $100.00 ($3.30) plus the $0.45 per-transaction processing fee — and you keep $96.25 before your item cost. There is no selling fee for US sellers: Depop removed its 10% selling fee on 18 July 2024. The one catch is the fee base: processing is charged on the item price plus any shipping the buyer pays and any applicable sales tax, so $8 of buyer-paid shipping raises the fee to $4.01. How the 3.3% + $0.45 works — and the shipping trap

Depop's US seller fee is one number: 3.3% plus $0.45 per transaction, charged by Depop Payments (via Stripe) on the item price plus shipping costs and any applicable taxes. Listing is free, so you pay only when something sells, and there is no separate payment-processing line on top of it — the 3.3% plus $0.45 is the processing fee. There is no separate PayPal fee for US sellers: Depop Payments is mandatory for US sales, and PayPal processing applies only to sellers outside the UK, US and Australia, who still pay the 10% selling fee too. See the Depop Fees Explained guide for the full fee structure in plain language.

The trap is that the shipping money goes to the carrier label, not to you — but Depop still takes 3.3% of it. A $100 item with $8.00 of buyer-paid shipping pays 3.3% of $108.00 plus $0.45 = $4.01, instead of $3.75. You keep $103.99 of the $108.00, but $8.00 of that is the shipping money for the label, so your real keep on the item is $95.99. Sales tax works the same way: the tax is not your money, but you still pay 3.3% on it. Offering free shipping shrinks the fee base to the item price alone — $3.75 on a $100 sale — but then the label cost comes out of your pocket. Which setup wins depends on your label cost and whether free shipping lets you charge a higher price, so run both through the Shipping Cost Comparison Calculator before you choose.

The worked $100 example: a jacket, bought for $30

You list a jacket for $100.00, a buyer buys it at full price with no shipping charged, and you sourced it for $30.00.

Amount
Sale price$100.00
Depop fee (3.3% x $100.00 + $0.45)$3.75
You keep (before item cost)$96.25
Item cost$30.00
Your profit$66.25

Check the maths. The fee is 3.3% × $100.00 = $3.30, plus $0.45 = $3.75. Subtract from the sale price: $100.00 − $3.75 = $96.25. Subtract your $30.00 item cost: $96.25 − $30.00 = $66.25 profit, a 66.25% margin on the sale price. Now the same jacket with $8.00 of buyer-paid shipping: the buyer pays $108.00, the fee is 3.3% × $108.00 + $0.45 = $4.01, you keep $103.99, and after the $8.00 label and your $30.00 item cost your profit is $65.99 — $0.26 less than the no-shipping version, because the fee also bites the shipping. The buyer also pays a Marketplace fee at checkout — up to 5% of the item price plus up to $1.00 — but that is added at checkout and does not come out of your $96.25; it does raise what the buyer actually pays, so factor it in when you compare your price against other marketplaces.

Why old Depop fee advice is wrong

Depop has changed its fee structure twice in recent years, and outdated advice is still everywhere. Until March 2024, UK sellers paid 10% and US sellers paid 10% too. Then Depop dropped the selling fee — in the UK in March 2024 and in the US on 18 July 2024 — and shifted costs to buyers through a Marketplace fee of up to 5% plus up to $1.00 at checkout. That structure is still in force. If you see "Depop takes 10% of your sale" advice, it is from before July 2024 and it is outdated for US sellers — and if a calculator still adds a 10% selling fee or a separate PayPal processing line to your US Depop sale, it is using the pre-2024 structure too. One newer change to watch: the Boosting fee rose from 8% to 12% for US and UK sellers on listings created from 23 March 2026.

What else can take a cut

The 3.3% plus $0.45 is the whole standard fee, but three things can still reduce your payout. Buyer-paid shipping grows the fee base, as the section above shows — $4.01 instead of $3.75 on a $100 sale with $8 shipping. Sales tax is collected from the buyer and remitted by Depop, but the 3.3% still applies to it, so a taxed sale costs you a few cents more in fees. Boosting is the big one: a sale through a Boosted listing created from 23 March 2026 costs an extra 12% of the item price — $12.00 on a $100 sale — taking the total to $15.75 and your keep to $84.25. The boost fee applies to shipping too if you used your own label instead of a Depop Shipping label, and the 28-day attribution window means a buyer who saw the boost can trigger the fee even after you turn boosting off. None of these is in the $3.75 above, so treat that figure as the fee, not the whole story. Compare platforms in the Seller Fees Comparison Calculator.

Depop vs the other marketplaces on $100

Depop's $3.75 is the cheapest fee in the $100 series by a wide margin — less than half of Etsy's $9.95 and under a fifth of Poshmark's $20.00. The gap only closes if you boost: a boosted Depop sale at $15.75 costs more than eBay's $14.00.

A $100 sale on Depop and the same sale on Mercari differ by $6.25 on the fee line — $3.75 against $10.00 — but Depop buyers pay a separate Marketplace fee at checkout and Mercari buyers pay the 3.6% protection fee, so compare the full cost, not just the fee, before you choose where to list. Frequently asked questions

How much does Depop take from a $100 sale?

Depop takes $3.75 from a $100 sale for a US seller — 3.3% of $100.00 ($3.30) plus the $0.45 per-transaction processing fee — and you keep $96.25 before your item cost. There is no selling fee for US sellers. The $3.75 assumes no buyer-paid shipping: processing is charged on the item price plus any shipping the buyer pays and any applicable sales tax, so $8 of buyer-paid shipping raises the fee to $4.01.

Does Depop still take 10% from US sellers?

No. Depop removed its 10% selling fee for US sellers on 18 July 2024. US, UK and Australian sellers now pay no selling fee at all — only the payment-processing fee of 3.3% plus $0.45 in the US. Sellers outside the UK, US and Australia still pay the 10% selling fee plus PayPal processing. Any guide that still adds a 10% selling fee to a US Depop sale is using the pre-July 2024 structure.

Does Depop charge a payment-processing fee?

Yes — and it is the only fee on a standard US sale. Depop Payments, run through Stripe, charges 3.3% plus $0.45 per transaction, on the item price plus shipping plus any applicable sales tax. There is no separate PayPal fee for US sellers: Depop Payments is mandatory for US sales, and PayPal processing applies only to sellers outside the UK, US and Australia.

Does Depop charge fees on shipping?

Yes — the 3.3% processing fee applies to shipping the buyer pays and to any applicable sales tax, not just the item price. A $100 item with $8.00 of buyer-paid shipping pays 3.3% of $108.00 plus $0.45, or $4.01, instead of $3.75. The shipping money itself pays for your label, so your real keep on the item is $95.99. Using a Depop Shipping label does not change the processing charge, but it does keep the 12% Boost fee off the shipping if the listing is boosted.

What is Depop's fee on a $50 sale?

Depop takes $2.10 from a $50 sale — 3.3% of $50.00 ($1.65) plus $0.45 — and you keep $47.90 before your item cost. On a $25 sale it takes $1.28 and you keep $23.72; on a $10 sale it takes $0.78 and you keep $9.22. All of these assume no buyer-paid shipping and no boost; shipping the buyer pays is added to the fee base.

Is Depop or Mercari cheaper on a $100 sale?

Depop is cheaper on the fee line. On a $100 sale Depop takes $3.75 — only the 3.3% plus $0.45 processing fee, with no selling fee for US sellers — and you keep $96.25, while Mercari takes $10.00 and you keep $90.00, a $6.25 difference. Depop buyers pay a separate Marketplace fee at checkout (up to 5% plus up to $1) and Mercari buyers pay a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee, so compare the full cost, not just the fee, before you choose where to list.
