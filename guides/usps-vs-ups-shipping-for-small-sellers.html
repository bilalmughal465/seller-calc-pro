USPS vs UPS Shipping for Small Sellers

Short answer: USPS usually wins on small, dense parcels under 1 cubic foot because it bills actual weight there, while UPS and FedEx bill dimensional weight from the first cubic inch. On light, bulky boxes, compare both quotes for the same box — dimensional weight can more than triple the billable weight.

How dimensional weight decides the price

Carriers charge the greater of actual weight and dimensional weight. Dimensional weight = (length × width × height in inches) ÷ divisor, with each side rounded up to the next whole inch first and the result rounded up to the next whole pound. UPS Daily Rates, FedEx and USPS use a divisor of 139; UPS Retail Rates use 166. USPS only applies dimensional weight above 1 cubic foot, so a parcel of 1,728 cubic inches or less bills on actual weight with USPS.

Worked example

An 18 × 14 × 12 inch box holding a 6 lb item: volume is 18 × 14 × 12 = 3,024 cubic inches. Dimensional weight at divisor 139 is 3,024 ÷ 139 = 21.76, rounded up to 22 lb. With UPS or FedEx, the parcel bills at 22 lb, not 6 lb. With USPS, the box is over 1 cubic foot (3,024 is greater than 1,728), so dimensional weight applies there too, and it also bills at 22 lb. Now shrink the box to 12 × 10 × 8 inches: volume is 960 cubic inches, under 1 cubic foot. Dimensional weight would be 960 ÷ 139 = 6.91, rounded up to 7 lb, but USPS bills the 6 lb actual weight because the parcel is under the threshold, while UPS and FedEx bill 7 lb. Fitting the smaller box saves a full billable pound on every carrier, and can save more on services where the weight break falls between 6 and 7 lb.

Comparing quotes fairly

Never compare a USPS quote for one box with a UPS quote for another. Measure the packed box, not the product, since padding and the box itself decide the billed size. Get both quotes for the same dimensions, weight and destination zone, then add pickup fees, residential surcharges and insurance to both sides before choosing. This guide deliberately gives no published rate table: carrier rates change by service, zone and account, so enter the quotes you actually receive rather than trusting a printed price.

When each carrier tends to win

USPS tends to win for parcels under 1 lb and for small, dense parcels, and its flat-rate boxes remove dimensional weight entirely for items that fit. UPS tends to win for heavier parcels, for multi-parcel shipments with an account rate, and where guaranteed delivery windows matter. FedEx sits between them on many lanes and is worth quoting on any parcel over a few pounds. The pattern to remember is not a winner, but a rule: the lighter and bulkier the parcel, the more the divisor matters, and the more quotes you should collect.

FAQs

What is dimensional weight?

Dimensional weight prices a parcel by the space it takes up rather than what it weighs. Carriers multiply length by width by height in inches and divide by a divisor, then bill the greater of that result and the actual weight.

What divisor do US carriers use?

UPS Daily Rates, FedEx and USPS use 139 cubic inches per pound for US domestic parcels. UPS Retail Rates, meaning UPS Store and counter prices, use 166. USPS moved to 139 on July 12, 2026, and only applies dimensional weight to parcels over 1 cubic foot (1,728 cubic inches).

When does USPS charge dimensional weight?

Only on parcels over 1 cubic foot for Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express, USPS Ground Advantage and Parcel Select. A small, dense parcel bills on actual weight with USPS even when UPS or FedEx would bill dimensional weight.

Why would UPS and USPS quotes differ on the same box?

Because they can bill different weights for the same parcel, and because their rate tables differ by service and zone. A light, bulky box that bills at 22 lb with one carrier and 6 lb with another will rarely price the same.

Should I always choose the cheaper quote?

Not automatically. Compare delivery speed, tracking, included insurance, pickup cost and return handling as well. The cheapest label that arrives late or without useful tracking can cost more in refunds and messages than it saved.
