How Much Does Facebook Marketplace Take From a $100 Sale? A Worked Walkthrough for Sellers

Short answer: Facebook Marketplace takes $10.00 from a $100 sale for a US seller — a 10% selling fee on the item price, with payment processing already included in that 10%. That $100 figure assumes no shipping and no tax: the fee is charged on the full transaction total, so a $100 item with $10 shipping costs $11.00 in fees, and local pickup costs $0 — you keep the whole $100. There is no listing fee and no subscription. The old 5% rate you may still see quoted in older guides is outdated. The formula, worked on $100

Facebook Marketplace fee = 10% × (item price + shipping charged to the buyer + sales tax collected), with a $0.80 minimum per shipped order. Payment processing is included in the 10% — there is no separate processing charge. Local pickup sales are $0: no fee at all.

Worked on $100 with no shipping or tax: 10% × $100.00 = $10.00. You keep $100.00 − $10.00 = $90.00 before your own costs — the item cost, the label and packaging, which are covered in the walkthrough below.

Worked walkthrough: buy a chair for $40, sell it for $100

You buy a used chair for $40.00 and sell it for $100.00 on Facebook Marketplace, charging the buyer $10.00 for shipping. This walkthrough uses several calculators' worth of maths in one place: the fee, the payout, and the profit after your own costs.

Check the maths. The fee base is the full $110.00 the buyer pays, not just the $100.00 item price — 10% × $110.00 = $11.00. The payout is $110.00 − $11.00 = $99.00. Subtract the $40.00 you paid for the chair, the $8.00 label and $2.00 of packaging, and the profit is $49.00. The same chair, sold for local pickup

Sell the same $100.00 chair for local pickup and the fee disappears entirely: $100.00 sale, $0.00 fee, you keep $100.00. Subtract the $40.00 you paid for the chair and your profit is $60.00 — $11.00 more than the shipped version, because there is no fee on the sale and no label or packaging to pay for. That $11.00 gap is the whole reason local pickup is the most profitable way to sell furniture and other bulky items on Marketplace: the fee is $0 and the shipping cost is $0. A $100 item with $10 shipping and $7.50 tax

Sales tax is part of the fee base too. A $100.00 item with $10.00 shipping and $7.50 of sales tax is a $117.50 total: the fee is 10% × $117.50 = $11.75, and the payout is $117.50 − $11.75 = $105.75. The tax is collected from the buyer and passed on, but the fee on it still comes out of your side — $0.75 of the $11.75 fee is the fee on the tax alone. If you had priced the same item at $100.00 with free shipping, the fee would be $10.00 — but you would pay the label yourself, so compare the label price first in the Shipping Cost Comparison Calculator before you choose. Common mistakes when working out Facebook Marketplace's fee

Quoting the old 5% rate. Facebook used to charge 5% with a $0.40 minimum. The current rate is 10% with a $0.80 minimum — pricing from 5% understates the fee by half. Forgetting the fee on shipping and tax. The fee is charged on the full transaction total, so a $100 item with $10 shipping is a $110 fee base, not $100. Assuming local pickup has a fee. It does not — local pickup is $0, which is why it beats shipping by $11.00 on the chair walkthrough above. Forgetting the $0.80 minimum on small sales. On a $5 sale the fee is $0.80, not $0.50 — 16% of the price, not 10%. Adding a separate payment processing fee. Processing is already inside the 10% on Marketplace; there is no second charge to add, unlike Etsy or Depop where processing is a separate line. Pricing from the item price only. The item cost, the label and packaging come out of the $99.00 payout, not out of the $110.00 the buyer paid — the walkthrough above shows a $49.00 profit, not $99.00. Method and limits

The standard rates are Meta's published US rules as reported in October–November 2026: a 10% selling fee per shipped checkout order with a $0.80 minimum, charged on the item price plus buyer-paid shipping plus sales tax collected; payment processing included in the 10%; local pickup sales free; no listing fee and no subscription. The fee table excludes shipping and sales tax, so on a shipped or taxed sale the fee is higher, as the sections above show. The comparison table is the fee on the $100 item price alone, before shipping and tax, and the eBay, Etsy, Poshmark, Mercari and Depop figures are the standard US rates from SellerCalc's own guides for those platforms. Non-US sellers may see different rules, and Meta can change its fees at any time — always check Meta's current seller pages before you price, promote or list.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Facebook Marketplace take from a $100 sale?

Facebook Marketplace takes $10.00 from a $100 sale for a US seller when no shipping is charged: a 10% selling fee on the $100 item price, with payment processing already included. If you charge the buyer $10.00 for shipping, the fee is 10% of the $110.00 total, which is $11.00, and you receive $99.00 before your own costs. Local pickup is free — a $100 local sale has a $0 fee and you keep the full $100.00.

Is there a fee for local pickup on Facebook Marketplace?

No. Local pickup sales on Facebook Marketplace have no fee at all — you keep the full sale price. The 10% selling fee applies only to shipped orders paid through Meta checkout. That is why a $100 chair sold for local pickup leaves you $100.00 before your own costs, while the same chair shipped with $10.00 of buyer-paid shipping leaves you $99.00 before your own costs.

Does Facebook Marketplace charge a fee on shipping?

Yes. The 10% selling fee is charged on the full transaction total: the item price plus the shipping you charge the buyer plus any sales tax collected. On a $100.00 item with $10.00 shipping, the fee is 10% of $110.00, which is $11.00. With $7.50 of sales tax on top, the total is $117.50 and the fee is $11.75. Shipping you pay yourself out of a free-shipping price is not added to the fee base, but it still comes out of your payout.

What is the minimum selling fee on Facebook Marketplace?

The minimum selling fee is $0.80 per shipped order. On a $5.00 sale, 10% would be $0.50, so the $0.80 minimum applies instead and you keep $4.20 — a 16% share. On an $8.00 sale, 10% is exactly $0.80, so you keep $7.20. The minimum only bites on sales under $8.00; above that, the straight 10% applies.

Is Facebook Marketplace cheaper than eBay on a $100 sale?

Yes, on a plain $100 sale with no shipping or tax. Facebook Marketplace takes $10.00 and you keep $90.00; eBay takes $14.00 in most categories (13.6% plus the $0.40 per-order fee) and you keep $86.00 — a difference of $4.00 in Facebook's favour. Local pickup widens the gap further, because Facebook charges $0 while eBay still charges its fee. Depop is cheaper still on a shipped $100 sale at $3.75 (3.3% + $0.45 processing only, no selling fee).

Did Facebook Marketplace used to charge 5%?

Yes. Facebook Marketplace used to charge a 5% selling fee with a $0.40 minimum. That rate is outdated — the current rate is 10% with a $0.80 minimum, charged on the full transaction total including shipping and sales tax. Any guide, video or calculator still quoting 5% is using the old rate: check Meta's current published fee schedule before you price or list.
