How Much Does Mercari Take From a $100 Sale? A Worked Example for Sellers

Short answer: Mercari takes $10.00 from a $100 sale — a flat 10% selling fee — and you keep $90.00 before your item cost. There is no separate payment-processing fee and no listing fee. The one catch is the fee base: the 10% is charged on the item price plus any shipping the buyer pays, so $8 of buyer-paid shipping raises the fee to $10.80. How the 10% works — and the shipping trap

Mercari's US seller fee is one number: 10% of the completed item price plus any shipping the buyer pays, in effect since 6 January 2025. Listing is free, so you pay only when something sells, and there is no separate payment-processing line — that fee was eliminated on the same date. See the Mercari Fees Explained guide for the full fee structure in plain language.

The trap is that the shipping money goes to the carrier label, not to you — but Mercari still takes 10% of it. A $100 item with $8.00 of buyer-paid shipping pays 10% of $108.00 = $10.80, an effective 10.8% of the item price. You keep $97.20 of the $108.00, but $8.00 of that is the shipping money for the label, so your real keep on the item is $89.20. Offer free shipping instead and the fee base shrinks to the item price alone — $10.00 on a $100 sale — but then the label cost comes out of your pocket. Which setup wins depends on your label cost and whether free shipping lets you charge a higher price, so run both through the Mercari Profit Calculator before you choose.

The worked $100 example: a jacket, bought for $30

You list a jacket for $100.00, a buyer buys it at full price with no shipping charged, and you sourced it for $30.00.

Amount
Sale price$100.00
Mercari fee (10% x $100.00)$10.00
You keep (before item cost)$90.00
Item cost$30.00
Your profit$60.00

Check the maths. The fee is 10% × $100.00 = $10.00. Subtract from the sale price: $100.00 − $10.00 = $90.00. Subtract your $30.00 item cost: $90.00 − $30.00 = $60.00 profit, a 60% margin on the sale price. The buyer also pays a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee — $3.60 on a $100 sale — but that is added at checkout and does not come out of your $90.00; it does raise what the buyer actually pays to $103.60, so factor it in when you compare your price against other marketplaces.

Why old Mercari fee advice is wrong

Mercari has changed its fee structure twice in under two years, and outdated advice is still everywhere. Until March 2024, sellers paid 10% plus payment processing. Then Mercari dropped seller fees to zero and shifted costs to buyers through service fees and a 2.9% plus $0.50 processing charge. That experiment lasted under a year: on 6 January 2025 Mercari reversed course, the 10% selling fee returned, buyer-side charges were consolidated into a smaller 3.6% Buyer Protection fee, and the separate payment-processing fee was eliminated for both sides. That structure is still in force. If you see "zero selling fees on Mercari" advice, it is from the March 2024 to January 2025 window and it is outdated — and if a calculator still adds a 2.9% processing line to your Mercari sale, it is using the pre-2025 structure too.

What else can take a cut

The 10% is the whole standard fee, but four things can still reduce your payout. Buyer-paid shipping grows the fee base, as the section above shows — $10.80 instead of $10.00 on a $100 sale with $8 shipping. Free shipping shrinks the fee base but puts the label on you. Instant Pay costs a flat $3.00 per cash-out to a debit card (up to $600 a month); standard direct deposit is free. Cancelling sales frequently can trigger a 5% cancellation fee, capped at $25. Mercari also runs a Seller Tiers program that grants selling-fee discounts to high-volume sellers, but the exact discounts are shown only inside your seller dashboard, so they are not modelled here. None of these is in the $10.00 above, so treat that figure as the fee, not the whole story. Compare platforms in the Seller Fees Comparison Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Mercari take from a $100 sale?

Mercari takes $10.00 from a $100 sale — a flat 10% selling fee — and you keep $90.00 before your item cost. There is no separate payment-processing fee and no listing fee. The $10.00 assumes no buyer-paid shipping: the fee is charged on the item price plus any shipping the buyer pays, so $8 of buyer-paid shipping raises the fee to $10.80.

What is Mercari's selling fee in 2026?

Mercari's US selling fee is a flat 10% of the item price plus any buyer-paid shipping, in effect since 6 January 2025. There is no separate payment-processing fee for sellers, no listing fee, and standard direct-deposit cash-out is free. Buyers separately pay a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee, which does not come out of your payout.

Does Mercari charge a payment-processing fee?

No. Mercari eliminated the separate seller payment-processing fee on 6 January 2025, when the 10% selling fee returned. Before that, sellers paid 2.9% plus $0.50 in processing on top of the fee. Any guide that still adds a processing line to a Mercari sale is using the pre-2025 structure.

Does Mercari's 10% fee apply to shipping?

Yes — the 10% applies to the item price plus any shipping the buyer pays, even though the shipping money goes to the carrier label, not to you. A $100 item with $8.00 of buyer-paid shipping pays 10% of $108.00, or $10.80, an effective 10.8% of the item price. Offering free shipping shrinks the fee base to the item price alone, but then the label cost comes out of your pocket.

What is Mercari's fee on a $50 sale?

Mercari takes $5.00 from a $50 sale — 10% of $50.00 — and you keep $45.00 before your item cost. On a $25 sale it takes $2.50 and you keep $22.50; on a $10 sale it takes $1.00 and you keep $9.00. All of these assume no buyer-paid shipping; shipping the buyer pays is added to the fee base.

Is Mercari or Depop cheaper on a $100 sale?

Depop is cheaper on the fee line. On a $100 sale Depop takes $3.75 — only the 3.3% plus $0.45 payment-processing fee, with no selling fee for US sellers — and you keep $96.25, while Mercari takes $10.00 and you keep $90.00, a $6.25 difference. Depop's processing is also charged on shipping the buyer pays, and Depop buyers pay a separate Marketplace fee at checkout, so compare the full cost, not just the fee, before you choose where to list.
