Poshmark vs eBay Fees: Which Keeps You More?

Short answer: eBay usually leaves you more once a sale is above about $25 to $30 — a $50 jacket keeps $40.00 on Poshmark and $41.24 on eBay, and a $100 item keeps $80.00 on Poshmark and $83.96 on eBay. Poshmark usually leaves you more between about $11 and $20, where its flat $2.95 fee under $15 and its buyer-paid shipping beat eBay's fee on the shipping and tax. The crossover is not a guess: the table below runs the same item through both platforms at five prices, with the shipping and tax that eBay charges its fee on shown in full. How each platform charges

Poshmark: the fee is simple — 20% of the sale price on sales of $15 or more, or a flat $2.95 under $15. The buyer pays a flat $6.49 for shipping and you get a prepaid label for parcels up to 5 lb, so shipping never touches your payout unless you offered a shipping discount or the parcel is overweight. The fee is charged on the sale price only — never on shipping or tax.

eBay: the fee is smaller but wider. The final value fee is 13.6% in most categories, charged on the total the buyer pays — the item price plus the shipping plus the sales tax — plus a per-order fee of $0.30 on orders of $10 or less and $0.40 over $10. You also buy the shipping label yourself, and the label cost comes out of what you receive. Listing is free up to 250 a month, then $0.35 per listing.

Read the table from the bottom up and the pattern is clear: the gap grows with the price. At $10 the two are 11 cents apart; at $50 eBay leaves $1.24 more; at $100 it leaves $3.96 more; at $200 it leaves $9.41 more. The crossover sits at about $25 to $30 — below it Poshmark's flat fee and free shipping win, above it eBay's lower percentage wins. Check the $50 row by hand: the buyer pays $50.00 + $8.00 shipping + $3.50 tax = $61.50; eBay takes 13.6% of $61.50 = $8.36 plus the $0.40 per-order fee = $8.76; you pay the $8.00 label and the $3.50 tax goes to the state, so you keep $61.50 − $8.76 − $8.00 − $3.50 = $41.24, against Poshmark's flat $40.00.

The shipping difference is the real difference

The fee percentages get the attention, but shipping decides more sales than the fee does. On Poshmark the buyer pays a flat $6.49 and you print a prepaid label — no weighing, no carrier choice, no label cost. On eBay you set the shipping, buy the label, and pay for it out of what you receive — and eBay charges its 13.6% on the shipping too, so a high shipping charge costs you twice. Heavy items are where this bites: a coat that costs $15 to ship leaves you $15 poorer on eBay before the fee, while on Poshmark the buyer pays the same $6.49 up to 5 lb and you pay a $5.00 upgrade only above it. The other side of the same coin: on Poshmark a buyer paying $6.49 shipping on a $10 top is paying 65% of the item's price in shipping, and that kills cheap sales — eBay lets you charge exact calculated shipping instead.

Which one fits your inventory

Pick Poshmark if you sell fashion — clothes, shoes, bags, beauty — and you want the simplest possible sale: list, share, ship with a prepaid label. Pick it for cheap pieces under $20, for bundles where one $6.49 label covers several items, and when you want offers and sharing to do the selling for you.

Pick eBay if you sell anything that is not fashion — electronics, collectibles, auto parts, tools, books — or anything over about $30, where the lower fee leaves you more. Pick it for rare or branded items where auctions and a worldwide buyer pool find the real price, and for heavy items where you can charge exact shipping instead of eating the difference.

Pick both if your inventory is mixed: cross-list the fashion on Poshmark and the rest on eBay, and price each listing for that platform's fee — the same $50 jacket needs a different list price on each platform to leave you the same money. Remove the item from the other platform the moment it sells, and check both apps after every sale so a double sale never happens.

Three mistakes that cost sellers on this comparison

Comparing 20% with 13.6% and stopping there. The percentages are not charged on the same amount. Poshmark's 20% is on the sale price only; eBay's 13.6% is on the item plus the shipping plus the tax, and then you still buy the label. Run the full row, not the headline rate.

Forgetting the label on eBay. The $8.00 label in the table above is real money out of your keep, and on a cheap item it is the biggest single cost after the fee. A $20 top on eBay keeps $15.60 only because the label is already counted — forget it and the sale looks $8.00 better than it is.

Forgetting Poshmark's flat $2.95 under $15. Under $15 Poshmark does not take 20% — it takes $2.95, which is 29.5% of a $10 sale. That is why the $10 row is nearly tied even though 20% sounds far worse than 13.6%. Bundle cheap items instead of selling them one by one — see the Poshmark seller mistakes guide for the bundle maths.

Frequently asked questions

Is Poshmark or eBay cheaper to sell on?

eBay is cheaper on most sales above about $25 to $30, because its 13.6% final value fee is well below Poshmark's 20%, even though eBay also charges that fee on shipping and sales tax and you buy the label. Poshmark usually leaves you more between about $11 and $20, where its flat $2.95 fee under $15 and its buyer-paid shipping beat eBay's fee on the shipping and tax. Below about $10 the two are within cents of each other.

How much does eBay take from a $50 sale?

On a $50 sale with $8 buyer-paid shipping and 7% sales tax, the buyer pays $61.50 in total. eBay takes 13.6% of that $61.50, which is $8.36, plus a $0.40 per-order fee, so $8.76 in fees. You also buy the $8.00 label, and the $3.50 tax goes to the state, so you keep $61.50 minus $8.76 minus $8.00 minus $3.50, which is $41.24.

How much does Poshmark take from a $50 sale?

Poshmark takes 20% of a $50 sale, which is $10.00, so you keep $40.00. The buyer pays the $6.49 flat shipping label separately, so shipping does not come out of your $40.00 unless you offered a shipping discount or the parcel weighs over 5 lb.

Does eBay charge fees on shipping and sales tax?

Yes. eBay charges its final value fee on the total sale amount — the item price plus the shipping the buyer pays plus the sales tax — even though eBay collects the tax and sends it to the state. That is the part most sellers forget: on a $50 sale with $8 shipping and 7% tax, the fee is 13.6% of $61.50, not 13.6% of $50.00.

Which is better for selling clothes, Poshmark or eBay?

Poshmark is better for fashion — clothes, shoes, bags and accessories — because its buyers are there for fashion, bundles and offers are built in, and the prepaid label means no shipping work. eBay is better for anything that is not fashion, for rare or branded items where auctions and a global buyer pool help, and for any item over about $30 where its lower fee leaves you more.

Can I sell the same item on Poshmark and eBay at the same time?

Yes, cross-listing is common: list the item on both platforms and remove it from the other one the moment it sells. The risk is a double sale — the same item selling on both platforms before you can remove it — so check both apps after every sale and price each listing for that platform's fee, because the same price keeps a different amount on each.
