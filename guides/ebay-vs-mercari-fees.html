eBay vs Mercari Fees: Which Keeps You More on the Same Sale?

Short answer: Mercari keeps you more on most ordinary sales. Mercari charges a flat 10% of the item price plus buyer-paid shipping, with no listing fee, no per-order fee and no separate processing fee. eBay charges 13.6% of the total amount of the sale in most categories, plus a $0.40 per-order fee on orders over $10. On the $50 sale worked out below, Mercari takes $5.80 and eBay takes $8.29 — Mercari keeps $2.49 more. But the fee is not the whole decision: eBay's much larger buyer base, auctions, and international reach can mean a sale happens at all, and Mercari's buyer pays a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee that eBay's buyer does not.

Stand: November 2026. Sources: eBay's and Mercari's published fee pages as reported by current seller guides. Fee rules change — check each platform's current fees page before you price. No sales-tax or income-tax advice is given here.

Worked example: the same $50 sale on both

A $50 item, $8 of shipping charged to the buyer, an $8 shipping label you buy, a $12 item cost and $1 packaging. Sales tax is left out of both sides here — eBay charges its fee on sales tax too, so including it would widen eBay's fee further; the comparison below is the cleanest like-for-like case. The full fee stacks match SellerCalc's existing guides: eBay Fees by Category and Mercari Fees Explained.

Buyer pays$50.00 + $8.00 shipping = $58.00$50.00 + $8.00 shipping + $2.09 Buyer Protection fee = $60.09
Platform fee13.6% x $58.00 = $7.89, + $0.40 per-order fee = $8.2910% x $58.00 = $5.80
You keep$58.00 - $8.29 - $12.00 item - $8.00 label - $1.00 packaging = $28.71$58.00 - $5.80 - $12.00 item - $8.00 label - $1.00 packaging = $31.20

Check the maths. eBay: 13.6% x $58.00 = $7.888, rounded to $7.89, plus the $0.40 per-order fee gives $8.29. Then $58.00 - $8.29 - $12.00 - $8.00 - $1.00 = $28.71. Mercari: 10% x $58.00 = $5.80, and $58.00 - $5.80 - $12.00 - $8.00 - $1.00 = $31.20. The gap is $31.20 - $28.71 = $2.49 in Mercari's favour — and note the buyer also pays $2.09 more on Mercari, so Mercari's cheaper seller fee is partly paid for by the buyer.

Same item, free shipping: the gap stays

Offer free shipping instead — the buyer pays $50, you pay the $8 label yourself. Now the fee base changes on both sides, because neither platform charges its percentage on shipping you pay for.

The gap is $24.00 - $21.80 = $2.20. Free shipping shrinks the gap slightly, because the fee base is smaller on both sides, but Mercari still wins. The lesson is the same either way: on an ordinary sale, eBay's higher percentage and per-order fee cost you roughly $2 on every $50.

Where eBay wins despite the higher fee

1. Categories with a lower final value fee. Authenticated athletic shoes of $150 or more pay 8% on eBay with no per-order fee — $12.00 on a $150 pair, against Mercari's 10%, which is $15.00. eBay Store subscriptions can also lower the final value fee in some categories. Check your exact category before assuming Mercari is cheaper.

2. An item that only sells where the buyers are. eBay's buyer base is far larger than Mercari's, and it is worldwide. A niche, high-value or collectible item that sits unsold on Mercari earns nothing, whatever the fee. The cheapest fee on a sale that never happens is still a loss.

3. Auctions and best offer. eBay lets an auction or a best-offer negotiation find a higher price than a fixed listing. A $5 higher sale price on eBay covers the $2.49 fee gap on the $50 example and leaves money over.

4. International buyers. Mercari is US and Japan only. If your buyer is anywhere else, eBay is the only one of the two that can take the order at all — note the extra 1.65% international fee eBay charges when you do not use eBay International Shipping.

Where Mercari wins

1. Simplicity. One flat 10%, the same in every category, no per-order fee, no listing fee, no processing fee. There is nothing to look up per category and nothing that changes with the order size.

2. Low-price and high-volume casual selling. On a $15 item with $5 buyer-paid shipping, eBay takes 13.6% x $20 = $2.72 + $0.40 = $3.12; Mercari takes 10% x $20 = $2.00. The per-order fee alone is a fifth of Mercari's whole fee at that size, and eBay's $0.35 listing fee after the first 250 a month adds up for volume sellers.

3. Final-sale policy. Mercari treats sales as final unless the item was misrepresented, which means fewer return disputes eating into the margin you just calculated. eBay buyers can request a return for any reason where the seller accepts returns.

Method and limits

Figures use the published US rules as reported in November 2026: eBay 13.6% of the total amount of the sale up to $7,500 per item, then 2.35% above, plus a $0.30 per-order fee on orders of $10 or less or $0.40 over $10, with the first 250 listings a month free and $0.35 after; Mercari a flat 10% of the item price plus buyer-paid shipping, no listing fee, no per-order fee, no separate processing fee since 6 January 2025, and a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee paid by the buyer. The worked examples exclude sales tax, ads or promoted listings, and any regulatory or currency-conversion charges, and treat the shipping label you buy as equal to the shipping you charge. Category rates, Store subscription pricing and international fees change — always check each platform's current fees page before you price.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper for sellers, eBay or Mercari?

On the headline fee, Mercari. Mercari charges a flat 10% of the item price plus buyer-paid shipping, with no listing fee and no per-order fee. eBay charges 13.6% of the total amount of the sale in most categories, plus a $0.40 per-order fee on orders over $10. On a $50 item with $8 of buyer-paid shipping, Mercari takes $5.80 and eBay takes $8.29 — Mercari keeps $2.49 more.

Does eBay charge its fee on shipping and sales tax?

Yes. eBay's final value fee is charged on the total amount of the sale: the item price, any handling charges, shipping the buyer pays, sales tax, and any other applicable fees. Mercari's 10% is charged on the item price plus buyer-paid shipping only — sales tax is collected and remitted by Mercari and is not part of the seller's fee base.

Does Mercari still charge a payment processing fee?

No, not on current listings. For listings created or updated on or after 6 January 2025, Mercari charges no separate payment processing fee — the flat 10% selling fee is the only seller percentage. The old 2.9% + $0.30 processing charge was removed in that change. Older guides that still list it are out of date.

What does the buyer pay extra on eBay and Mercari?

On Mercari, the buyer pays a 3.6% Buyer Protection fee on the item price plus buyer-paid shipping, on top of the item price and shipping — on a $50 item with $8 shipping that is $2.09, so the buyer pays $60.09 before sales tax. On eBay, the buyer pays no platform fee: item price plus shipping plus sales tax only. Mercari's cheaper seller fee is partly paid for by the buyer, which can make a buyer choose the cheaper total elsewhere.

Is listing free on eBay and Mercari?

Listing on Mercari is always free, in any category and at any volume. On eBay, the first 250 listings each month are free; after that, each additional listing costs $0.35, and the insertion fee is non-refundable even if the item does not sell. A seller listing more than 250 items a month pays listing fees on eBay and none on Mercari.

When is eBay cheaper than Mercari?

In categories with a lower final value fee. The clearest case is authenticated athletic shoes of $150 or more, where eBay charges 8% with no per-order fee: on a $150 pair that is $12.00, against Mercari's 10%, which is $15.00. eBay Store subscriptions can also lower the final value fee in some categories. Outside those cases, Mercari's flat 10% is usually the cheaper fee on an ordinary sale.
