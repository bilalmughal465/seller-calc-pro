Whatnot First Show Checklist: Everything to Prepare Before You Go Live

Short answer: a first Whatnot show needs four things ready before you go live: seller approval confirmed, 30–50 items pre-listed with starting bids set, a tested phone-and-light setup, and a shipping plan for the orders you make. Price the items with the fees in mind — Whatnot takes 8% of the item price plus 2.9% of the order total plus $0.30 — and expect a small room: 5–15 viewers and a modest first night are normal, not a failure. Work through the checklist below in order, and the show mostly takes care of itself.

Stand: November 2026. Sources: Whatnot's published seller help pages and current seller guides as reported in November 2026. Fee rules and approval steps change — check Whatnot's current seller pages before you price or schedule. No sales-tax or income-tax advice is given here.

One week before: approval, category, schedule

  • ☐ Seller approval applied for — and confirmed. Whatnot requires approval before you can sell. You will need a government ID, bank or payout details, and photos of sample inventory. Seller guides report approval typically taking 3–7 days, so apply a week ahead and do not schedule a show until it is confirmed.
  • ☐ One category picked. Pick one category for the whole first show — trading cards, vintage clothing, sneakers, or whatever you know best. Whatnot promotes your show to buyers interested in that category, so mixing three categories in one show reaches nobody well.
  • ☐ Show scheduled 3–5 days ahead. Scheduling early gives Whatnot time to promote the show and gives you time to tell people about it. Pick an evening slot when your buyers are online — most categories do best between 6 and 10 PM Eastern.
  • ☐ Title and description written. Say exactly what you are selling and the starting price range. "Pokémon Vintage Lots + Modern Hits | $1 Auctions Starting" tells a buyer far more than "My First Show!" Add your free-shipping threshold if you have one, for example "3+ items = free shipping".

Three days before: inventory and pricing

  • ☐ 30–50 items ready. That is the right amount for a 1–2 hour show: enough variety to keep bidding moving even when some items get no bids. Fewer than 20 items and you run out in about 20 minutes.
  • ☐ Every item photographed. Clear photos, good light, a clean background, and extra angles for valuable items. Photos are also what Whatnot asks for in listings, so this is not optional polish — it is part of the listing.
  • ☐ Starting bids set — low on purpose. A common framework: $1 starting bid on items worth under $10, $1–$5 on items worth $10–$30, $5–$15 on items worth $30–$75, and higher only above that. Your first show is about volume, reviews and learning the app, not maximum profit per item.
  • ☐ Items pre-listed in the Whatnot app. Load every item before show day, in show order. Pre-listing saves roughly 30 seconds per item during the show — over 30 items, that is 15 minutes of dead air avoided.
  • ☐ Items organised in show order. Start with eye-catching, low-priced items to get early bids flowing, save your best items for 30–45 minutes in when viewer count peaks, and close with a few strong items so people stay to the end.

Worked example: what a $15 sale actually keeps

Whatnot charges two lines on every sale: an 8% commission on the item price only, plus a payment processing fee of 2.9% of the total order value plus $0.30 per order. The full fee stack is worked out in Whatnot Seller Fees Explained — here is what it means for your first show's pricing. Take a $15 item with $5 of buyer-paid shipping, and suppose you paid $6 for the item and $4 for the shipping label.

Amount
Buyer pays$15.00 item + $5.00 shipping = $20.00
Commission8% x $15.00 = $1.20
Processing2.9% x $20.00 = $0.58, + $0.30 = $0.88
Total Whatnot fees$1.20 + $0.88 = $2.08
You keep$15.00 - $2.08 - $6.00 item - $4.00 label = $2.92

Check the maths. Commission: 8% x $15.00 = $1.20. Processing: 2.9% x $20.00 = $0.58, plus the $0.30 per-order fee gives $0.88. Total fees: $1.20 + $0.88 = $2.08. Then $15.00 - $2.08 - $6.00 - $4.00 = $2.92. Now scale it to the whole show: if you sell 20 items at that same $15, gross sales are $300.00, fees are 20 x $2.08 = $41.60, and you keep $300.00 - $41.60 = $258.40 before item and label costs. Run your own numbers first in the Whatnot Fee Calculator — a $1 starting bid is a trap if you have not done this maths: on a $1 sale with $5 buyer-paid shipping, the commission is $0.08 and the processing fee is 2.9% x $6.00 = $0.17 plus $0.30, which is $0.47 — $0.55 in fees on a $1 item, leaving $0.45 before your label. Use $1 starts to build bidding energy, not as your whole pricing plan.

Equipment: the budget setup is enough

  • ☐ Phone with a decent camera. Your phone is fine for a first show — no camera purchase needed.
  • ☐ Tripod or phone mount. A steady camera is non-negotiable; shaky handheld video looks amateur and tires viewers.
  • ☐ Ring light. Good lighting is the single biggest quality upgrade. A basic 10-inch ring light costs little and transforms how your items look on camera.
  • ☐ Clean background. A blank wall or simple backdrop, free, and it keeps the focus on the item, not your room.
  • ☐ Phone charged to 100%, charger nearby. A dead phone ends the show — charge fully and keep the charger within reach.
  • ☐ Internet backup ready. A mobile hotspot covers you if your Wi-Fi drops mid-show. Test your upload speed beforehand; you need roughly 10 Mbps upload for smooth HD streaming.

Show day: the hour-by-hour plan

  • ☐ 3 hours before: set up the streaming area, lay out items in show order within arm's reach, set up a numbered bin or bag system for sold items, test internet speed, charge the phone.
  • ☐ 1 hour before: do a test stream, check audio levels, check lighting and framing, put water within reach — you will be talking nonstop.
  • ☐ 15 minutes before: queue your first 5 items in the Whatnot app, post on your other channels that you are going live in 15 minutes, and take a breath.
  • ☐ Opening 5 minutes: welcome viewers, say what you are selling and how many items, mention the free-shipping threshold, and ask new viewers to follow. Then start the first item — do not wait for a crowd.

During the show: keep it moving

  • ☐ Read chat constantly and answer by name. Chat is half the experience on Whatnot. A quick "Hey, welcome!" keeps people watching; silence makes them leave.
  • ☐ Narrate every item. Say what it is, its condition, any wear or damage, and why it is worth a bid. Never hold an item up silently.
  • ☐ Move on fast. If an item gets no bids after 30–45 seconds, move to the next one. "This one's not finding a home tonight — let's see what's next!" keeps the energy up.
  • ☐ Mention the free-shipping threshold 3–4 times. In the opening, when someone wins their second item, and in the last 15 minutes. Free shipping is the main driver of multi-item purchases on Whatnot, and buyers cannot act on a threshold they never heard.
  • ☐ Celebrate wins by name. "Nice grab, Sarah!" costs nothing and makes the buyer come back for show two.

After the show: shipping and the next one

  • ☐ Consolidate orders the same evening. Group each buyer's items together and confirm bin assignments while the show is fresh. Do not wait until tomorrow.
  • ☐ Print labels and pack. Batch-print labels and pack assembly-line style. Ship within two business days — same-day or next-day shipping earns the first reviews that bring viewers to show two.
  • ☐ Thank your buyers. A short message — "Thanks for joining tonight, your order is packed and shipping tomorrow" — sets expectations and makes buyers feel valued.
  • ☐ Write down the numbers. Total revenue, items sold, peak and average viewers, which items got the most bids, which got none, what felt slow, what felt great. That list is your plan for show two.
  • ☐ Schedule show two within a week. Consistency beats perfection. A two-week gap means your first audience forgets you; a few days means they are excited to come back.

Common first-show mistakes to avoid

1. Starting without enough inventory. 15 items for an hour-long show means you run out in 20 minutes. Bring 30–50.

2. Pricing too high. Your first show is not the time to maximise per-item profit. Price low, move volume, collect reviews — a seller with 50 five-star reviews gets far more viewers than a seller with zero history and "fair market" prices.

3. Ignoring chat. If someone says hi and you do not respond, they leave. Whatnot is a social platform first.

4. Bad audio. Viewers tolerate mediocre video. They will not tolerate bad audio. Test it before every show; a $30 lavalier microphone is the best early investment.

5. No shipping system. Selling 30 items is exciting until you have to pack and ship 30 items. Have supplies, bins and labels ready before the show, not after it.

Frequently asked questions

How many items do I need for my first Whatnot show?

Aim for 30 to 50 items for a 1 to 2 hour show. That is enough variety to keep the show moving even if some items get no bids. Showing up with 15 items for an hour-long show means you run out in about 20 minutes and have to end early — leftovers are better than dead air.

How long does Whatnot seller approval take?

Apply well before your planned show date. Whatnot requires seller approval before you can go live, and seller guides report approval typically taking 3 to 7 days. You will need a government ID, bank or payout details, and photos of sample inventory. Do not schedule a show until approval is confirmed.

What should I start bidding at on my first Whatnot show?

Start low: $1 starting bids on items worth under $10, $1 to $5 on items worth $10 to $30, and higher only on higher-value items. Your first show is about volume, reviews and learning the app — not maximum profit per item. But check the fee maths first: on a $1 sale with $5 buyer-paid shipping, Whatnot takes $0.55 in fees, so you keep $0.45 before your shipping label.

How much does Whatnot take from a sale?

Two lines. An 8% commission on the item price only, plus a payment processing fee of 2.9% of the total order value plus $0.30 per order. On a $15 item with $5 buyer-paid shipping, that is $1.20 commission plus $0.88 processing — $2.08 in total — so you keep $12.92 before your item cost and shipping label.

Do I need a second phone or a microphone for my first Whatnot show?

No. A phone with a decent camera, a tripod or mount, a ring light, and a clean background are enough. Viewers tolerate average video but leave fast when they cannot hear you, so test your audio before you go live — a cheap lavalier microphone is the one upgrade worth buying early. A second device for reading chat is helpful, not required.

How fast do I have to ship after a Whatnot sale?

Within two business days. Have your shipping supplies, bins for sold items, and a label printer or printer ready before the show, and consolidate and pack orders the same evening while the show is fresh. Same-day or next-day shipping is what earns the first reviews that bring viewers to show two.

Can I do my first Whatnot show with no followers?

Yes. A first show with 5 to 15 viewers and $50 to $300 in sales is normal, and every established Whatnot seller started there. Schedule the show 3 to 5 days ahead so Whatnot can promote it, tell people on your other channels, and judge the show by items sold and lessons learned — not by viewer count.
