eBay vs Facebook Marketplace Fees: Which Keeps You More on a $50 Sale?

Short answer: Facebook Marketplace is cheaper on fees — $0 on local pickup, and 10% on shipped orders versus eBay's 13.6% plus a $0.40 per-order fee. On a $50 item with $8 buyer-paid shipping, eBay takes $8.29 and Facebook takes $5.80, so Facebook keeps $2.49 more. But the fee winner is not always the profit winner: eBay's bigger, more targeted buyer pool can get a higher sale price for the same item, especially for niche or collectible items — and a $15 higher sale price wipes out the fee gap completely. Use the tables below to decide by item, not by habit.

Stand: November 2026. Sources: eBay's published selling-fees pages and Meta's Marketplace Seller Protection Policy, as reported by seller guides in October–November 2026. Fee rules change — check each platform's current pages before you list. No sales-tax or income-tax advice is given here.

Same item, same price, three ways to sell it. The buyer pays $50.00 + $8.00 shipping = $58.00 in every case, and the label costs you $8.00. Sales tax is left out of this example — both platforms charge their fee on tax too, so a taxed sale costs slightly more on both sides.

Check the maths. eBay: 13.6% x $58.00 = $7.888, rounded to $7.89; add the $0.40 per-order fee for $8.29 total. Payout: $58.00 - $8.29 = $49.71, and $49.71 - $8.00 label = $41.71. Facebook shipped: 10% x $58.00 = $5.80; payout $58.00 - $5.80 = $52.20, and $52.20 - $8.00 label = $44.20. Facebook keeps $44.20 - $41.71 = $2.49 more than eBay on the shipped sale. Local pickup beats both: $50.00 in hand, no fee, no label — $8.29 more than eBay and $5.80 more than shipped Facebook on this sale.

When eBay still wins despite the higher fee

1. The sale price is higher on eBay. Niche, collectible and brand-specific items routinely sell for 10–40% more on eBay because the buyer pool is national and searching for that exact item. If the $50 item sells for $65 on eBay, the extra $15 dwarfs the $2.49 fee gap.

2. There is no local buyer. A rare part, a discontinued model or a niche collectible may have fewer than a handful of interested buyers in your whole city. Facebook's local-first feed cannot find them; eBay's search can.

3. You need a paper trail. eBay gives documented payments, tracking and a dispute process. For high-value or high-fraud-risk items, that record is worth paying for.

4. You are scaling, not clearing out. eBay has stores, branding, promotions, analytics and 250 free listings a month. Facebook Marketplace has no store system, no branding and no real analytics — it is built for individual sales, not a growing business.

The $0.80 minimum: the trap on cheap shipped items

Facebook's $0.80 minimum only matters on tiny orders — but when it bites, it bites hard. A $5 shipped item with $3 shipping is an $8.00 order, so the fee is exactly $0.80, which is 10%. A $4 item with $2 shipping is a $6.00 order: 10% would be $0.60, so Facebook charges the $0.80 minimum instead — an effective rate of 13.3% before you buy a label. Cheap items belong on local pickup, not shipped checkout. eBay has the same shape of problem at the other end: its $0.40 per-order fee is a bigger share of a $12 sale than of a $120 one. Method and limits

Fee figures use the platforms' published US rules as reported in October–November 2026. eBay: a 13.6% final value fee on the total sale amount in most categories, in place since 14 February 2025, plus a $0.40 per-order fee on orders over $10 ($0.30 on $10 or less); some categories charge different rates, Store subscribers pay slightly less, and 250 listings a month are free before the $0.35 insertion fee. Facebook Marketplace: no fee on local pickup paid in person; shipped orders through checkout pay 10% of the whole transaction (item, shipping and tax) with a $0.80 minimum, in place since 15 April 2024 when it replaced the old 5% / $0.40 rate — that is what Meta's Seller Protection Policy states as checked in October 2026. One caution: a few pages still claim US shipped orders are fee-free since June 2024; the 10% figure is used here because it is what Meta's governing seller policy states and what the majority of current seller guides report. The worked examples exclude sales tax, so on a taxed sale both platforms' fees would be slightly higher than shown. Payout timing, buyer-pool sizes and return rules are as reported by the platforms and current seller guides. Always check each platform's current help pages before you price or list.

Frequently asked questions

Does Facebook Marketplace charge sellers a fee?

Local pickup paid in person carries no Facebook selling fee — the money never goes through Facebook's checkout. Shipped orders paid through Facebook checkout cost 10% of the whole transaction (item price, shipping the buyer pays, and sales tax), with a $0.80 minimum per order. That 10% rate has applied since 15 April 2024, when it replaced the old 5% rate.

How much does eBay take from a $50 sale?

On a $50 item with $8 of buyer-paid shipping, eBay charges 13.6% of the $58 total sale — $7.89 — plus a $0.40 per-order fee, for $8.29 in total fees. Your payout is $49.71 before you pay for the shipping label. The 13.6% rate applies in most categories; some categories charge a different rate.

Is Facebook Marketplace cheaper than eBay for sellers?

On fees alone, yes — local pickup is free, and shipped orders cost 10% versus eBay's 13.6% plus $0.40. On a $50 item with $8 shipping, Facebook Marketplace keeps $2.49 more than eBay. But eBay can still net you more if its larger, more targeted buyer pool gets a higher sale price for the same item — especially for niche or collectible items, where a $50 item might sell for $65 on eBay and only $50 locally.

What is the minimum fee on Facebook Marketplace?

$0.80 per shipped order. The minimum only bites when 10% of the order total comes to less than $0.80 — that is, on orders under $8 including shipping and tax. A $4 item with $2 shipping is a $6.00 order, so 10% would be $0.60 — Facebook charges the $0.80 minimum instead, an effective rate of 13.3% before you buy a label.

Does eBay charge a fee on shipping?

Yes. eBay's final value fee is charged on the total sale amount — the item price plus the shipping the buyer pays plus sales tax — not just the item price. That is why a $50 item with $8 shipping is charged on $58, not $50. Facebook Marketplace's shipped fee works the same way: 10% of the whole transaction, including shipping and tax.

Which platform pays sellers faster?

eBay is faster for shipped sales: payouts are usually initiated the day after the sale and arrive in 1 to 2 business days. Facebook Marketplace shipped payouts start 15 days after you mark the item shipped, or 5 days after confirmed delivery, whichever comes first — then up to 5 more days to reach your bank. Local pickup is instant: cash or a payment app, paid in person.

Can I list the same item on eBay and Facebook Marketplace?

Yes. List it on both and let speed and price decide — mid-value items between $30 and $100 often sell on either. But remove the listing from the other platform the moment it sells on one. Selling an item you no longer have damages your reputation and your seller metrics on whichever platform you fail to fulfil.
