How Much Does Amazon Take From a $100 Sale? A Worked Example for Sellers

Short answer: on a typical $100 FBA sale in a 15% category, Amazon takes about $20.49 and you keep about $79.51 before your product cost. That $20.49 is three lines: a $15.00 referral fee, a $5.30 FBA fulfilment fee, and a $0.19 fuel and logistics surcharge. If you fulfil the order yourself (FBM), Amazon takes only the $15.00 referral fee and you keep $85.00 before the plan fee. How the referral fee works

The referral fee is Amazon's charge for selling on its marketplace, and it applies to every sale — FBA or FBM, Individual or Professional plan. It is 15% in most categories, ranging from 8% to 20% depending on the category, with a $0.30 minimum per item. The fee base is the total sales price: the item price plus any shipping you charge the buyer plus gift wrap. So if you sell an item for $100.00 and charge the buyer $5.00 for shipping, the fee is 15% of $105.00, which is $15.75 — not $15.00. Free shipping avoids that extra charge, but you pay the label yourself instead. See the Amazon FBA Fees Explained guide for the full fee stack in plain language.

FBA vs FBM: what changes

The referral fee is the same either way. What changes is everything after it. With FBA, Amazon stores your stock, picks, packs and ships each order, handles Prime delivery and customer service on the order — and charges a fulfilment fee per unit plus a fuel and logistics surcharge on that fee, plus storage. With FBM, you store the stock and ship each order yourself, so you pay no fulfilment fee, no surcharge and no Amazon storage — but you pay the label, the packaging and your own time. On the worked $100 sale below, FBA costs $20.49 in Amazon fees and FBM costs $15.00 on the Professional plan — a difference of $5.49 per sale. The worked $100 example: FBA, Home & Kitchen, non-peak 2026

You sell a Home & Kitchen item for $100.00, large standard size, 1 to 1.25 lb, fulfilled by Amazon at non-peak 2026 rates, in a 15% referral-fee category.

Amount
Sale price$100.00
Referral fee (15% x $100.00)$15.00
FBA fulfilment fee (large standard, 1–1.25 lb)$5.30
Fuel and logistics surcharge (3.5% x $5.30)$0.19
Amazon takes$20.49
You keep (before product cost)$79.51

Check the maths. The referral fee is 15% × $100.00 = $15.00. The surcharge is 3.5% × $5.30 = $0.19. Add the three lines: $15.00 + $5.30 + $0.19 = $20.49. Subtract from the sale price: $100.00 − $20.49 = $79.51. That $79.51 is before your product cost, inbound shipping to Amazon and storage — the profit comes only after those.

Peak-season note: during the holiday peak, 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027, the fulfilment fee for the same item rises to $5.60 and the surcharge to $0.20 (3.5% of $5.60). The total Amazon cut becomes $15.00 + $5.60 + $0.20 = about $20.80, and you keep about $79.20. A $100 sale in the peak season costs you $0.31 more than the same sale outside it.

The same $100 sale, fulfilled by merchant (FBM)

Sell the same $100.00 item yourself and the fulfilment fee and the surcharge disappear. On the Professional plan, Amazon takes only the $15.00 referral fee and you keep $85.00 before the plan fee. On the Individual plan, you also pay $0.99 per item sold: Amazon takes $15.00 + $0.99 = $15.99 and you keep $84.01. The Professional plan costs $39.99 a month with no per-item fee, so it starts saving you money once you sell about 41 items a month — 41 × $0.99 is $40.59, which is more than the $39.99 monthly fee. Below that, the Individual plan is cheaper. The FBM saving against FBA is $5.49 per sale on this example, but you still have to buy the label and packaging and do the packing yourself — compare the label first in the Shipping Cost Comparison Calculator. What else can take a cut

The referral fee, the fulfilment fee and the surcharge are the core stack, but four more charges can appear. Storage fees are charged monthly on the space your stock occupies in Amazon's warehouses, and rise in the peak season. Inbound placement fees apply when you ship stock to Amazon's fulfilment centres. The low-inventory-level fee applies to FBA items whose stock runs too low relative to recent sales. Advertising — Sponsored Products and similar — is optional, but many sellers spend on it to win the Buy Box, and it comes straight out of the $79.51. None of these is in the $20.49 above, so treat that figure as the floor, not the ceiling. The FBA Storage Fee Calculator shows what storage adds on your own stock.

Method and limits

The figures are Amazon's published 2026 US rates for a standard-size item in a 15% referral-fee category: a 15% referral fee with a $0.30 minimum, charged on the item price plus buyer-paid shipping plus gift wrap; a $5.30 non-peak fulfilment fee for a large standard-size item of 1 to 1.25 lb, rising to $5.60 in the 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027 holiday peak; a 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge on the fulfilment fee; an Individual plan at $0.99 per item and a Professional plan at $39.99 per month. Your category, size tier and weight change the total — a small standard item costs less to fulfil than a large one, and a category at 8% or 20% changes the referral fee. Frequently asked questions

How much does Amazon take from a $100 sale?

On a typical $100 FBA sale in a 15% category, Amazon takes about $20.49: a $15.00 referral fee (15% of $100.00), a $5.30 fulfilment fee for a large standard-size item of 1 to 1.25 lb at non-peak 2026 rates, and a $0.19 fuel and logistics surcharge (3.5% of the fulfilment fee). You keep about $79.51 before your product cost. If you fulfil the order yourself (FBM) on the Professional plan, Amazon takes only the $15.00 referral fee and you keep $85.00 before the plan fee.

What percentage does Amazon take from a sale?

Amazon takes 15% in most categories, with the referral fee ranging from 8% to 20% depending on the category, and a $0.30 minimum per item. The fee is charged on the total sales price — the item price plus any shipping you charge the buyer plus gift wrap — not just the item price. On a $100 sale in a 15% category the referral fee is $15.00.

Does Amazon take a cut if I ship the order myself?

Yes. The referral fee applies on every sale, however you fulfil it. Shipping the order yourself (FBM) only removes the FBA fulfilment fee and the surcharge: on a $100 sale in a 15% category, Amazon still takes the $15.00 referral fee, and you keep $85.00 before the plan fee. On the Individual plan you also pay $0.99 per item, so Amazon takes $15.99 and you keep $84.01.

Is FBA or FBM cheaper on a $100 sale?

FBM is cheaper on the fee line alone. On the worked $100 Home and Kitchen example in this guide, FBA costs $20.49 in Amazon fees ($15.00 referral fee, $5.30 fulfilment fee, $0.19 surcharge) while FBM on the Professional plan costs $15.00 (the referral fee only) — a difference of $5.49 per sale. FBA buys you Prime delivery, Amazon handling the packing and shipping, and customer service on the order, so the cheaper fee is not automatically the better deal.

Should I choose the Individual or Professional selling plan?

The Individual plan costs $0.99 per item sold with no monthly fee; the Professional plan costs $39.99 per month with no per-item fee. The Professional plan starts saving you money once you sell about 41 items a month, because 41 times $0.99 is $40.59, which is more than the $39.99 monthly fee. Below that, the Individual plan is cheaper. On a single $100 FBM sale, the Individual plan leaves you $84.01 after Amazon's $15.99, while the Professional plan leaves you $85.00 before the monthly fee.

What is the FBA fulfilment fee on a $100 sale?

The fulfilment fee depends on the item's size tier and weight, not its price. For a large standard-size item of 1 to 1.25 lb, the non-peak 2026 rate is $5.30, plus a 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge of $0.19. During the holiday peak season, 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027, the fulfilment fee for the same item rises to $5.60 and the surcharge to $0.20, taking the total Amazon cut on a $100 sale to about $20.80. Most standard-size items cost $3 to $6 to fulfil in 2026, before the surcharge.
